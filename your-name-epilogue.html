Your Name — A Little More Time

Three chapters imagining the days after one simple question.

This is an unofficial, non-canonical fan-made continuation written for personal enjoyment. It begins immediately after the film's final encounter: Taki and Mitsuha do not recover their memories, do not remember their body-swapping, and do not know why the other feels familiar. They only have the chance to meet as two strangers who feel they should not remain strangers.
Chapter One

Two Strangers, One More Minute

“Your name?”

The question hung between them. The young woman blinked, as though she had only just realized she had been holding her breath.

“Mitsuha,” she answered. “Mitsuha Miyamizu.”

“I'm Taki. Taki Tachibana.”

They repeated the names quietly, almost testing how they sounded. Taki. Mitsuha. There was no sudden flash of recognition, no missing memory returning, no explanation for the strange ache that had brought them to that staircase. There was only the fact that neither wanted to walk away.

“Do you live around here?” Taki asked.

“Not far. What about you?”

“A train ride away.”

A pause followed. It was an ordinary question, an ordinary answer. Somehow, both of them were nervous.

“This might sound strange,” Mitsuha began, “but… would you mind if we talked for a little longer?”

Taki's answer came quickly. “I was about to ask you the same thing.”

They left the stairs and found a small café nearby. At first, they spoke about safe things: the weather, the city, the difficulty of finding a quiet place on a weekend. Their conversation stumbled now and then, but neither seemed bothered by it.

When the server brought their drinks, Mitsuha looked at Taki over the rim of her cup.

“Have we met somewhere before?”

Taki considered the question. “I don't think so. But I had the same feeling when I saw you.”

“It's frustrating, isn't it? Feeling like you should know someone, but having no idea why.”

“Maybe we just have one of those faces.”

She gave him a doubtful look.

“That was a terrible explanation,” he admitted.

“It really was.”

They laughed, and the awkwardness loosened.

They talked until the afternoon light began to change. Taki learned that Mitsuha liked quiet places and had a habit of looking at the sky when she was thinking. Mitsuha learned that Taki was studying, worked part-time, and was surprisingly bad at choosing what to order.

When they finally stood outside the café, the city seemed louder than before.

“I should go,” Mitsuha said. “My family will wonder where I am.”

“Right. Of course.”

Neither moved immediately.

Taki took out his phone. “Would it be okay if I asked for your number?”

Mitsuha smiled. “I was wondering when you'd ask.”

They exchanged contacts. No promises about destiny, no dramatic declarations. Just a message sent while they were still standing a few steps apart.

It's Taki. So you have my number too.

Her phone chimed. She typed a reply.

Got it. Don't lose mine.

“I won't,” he said.

She started toward the station, then turned back once. “It was nice meeting you, Taki.”

“You too, Mitsuha.”

For the first time that day, their goodbye felt less like an ending and more like an invitation.

Chapter Two

A Familiar Stranger

Their first messages were polite. The second day, they were less so. By the third, Mitsuha had already learned that Taki sometimes replied late because he forgot to check his phone, and Taki had learned that Mitsuha used far too many exclamation marks when she was excited.

They met again the following weekend, this time by the entrance to a bookshop. Mitsuha arrived first. Taki spotted her looking at the window display and approached quietly.

“You came early.”

She turned. “You sound surprised.”

“I was expecting you to make me wait.”

“I can still leave.”

“Please don't.”

She laughed and they went inside.

They spent the afternoon wandering through shops, sharing snacks, and talking about the little things that made up their lives. They did not try to solve the strange feeling between them. Whenever it surfaced, they let it pass without forcing an answer.

At one point, a familiar tune drifted from a shop speaker. Mitsuha stopped walking. For a second she felt a tug of emotion she couldn't explain—something like nostalgia for a place she couldn't picture.

Taki noticed. “Are you okay?”

“Yeah. That song just feels… familiar.”

“I know what you mean.”

Neither could say where they had heard it. The feeling faded as quickly as it had come.

Later, they sat by a window with warm drinks. Taki watched the people passing outside.

“Can I tell you something without making it weird?”

“That depends on what it is.”

“When we met on the stairs, I felt relieved. I don't know why. It was like I had been looking for someone, but I couldn't have told you who.”

Mitsuha looked down at her cup. “I felt that too.”

“It's a little embarrassing.”

“A little.”

“You could at least pretend to disagree.”

She smiled. “I think it's nice. We don't have to understand it right away.”

Taki nodded. He liked that answer.

They began meeting more often. Sometimes they planned an entire afternoon; sometimes it was only a short walk after one of them finished work or class. They discovered small differences, too. Mitsuha liked to plan ahead. Taki was better at improvising. She teased him for being indecisive about food. He teased her for taking photographs of every pretty sky.

One evening, Mitsuha sent him a picture of the sunset.

The sky looked too pretty not to share.

He replied with a photograph from his own window. The colors were already fading, but a thin line of orange remained between the buildings.

Same sky, I guess.

She looked at the message for a while. It was a simple sentence. Still, it made her smile.

They were not recovering a lost story. They were making a new one, one ordinary day at a time.

Chapter Three

A Day to Remember

By the time the next month arrived, Taki had stopped wondering whether it was too soon to ask Mitsuha to spend an entire day with him. He had a plan, though he had rewritten the message several times before finally sending it.

Are you free this Saturday? I'd like to take you somewhere.

Her reply came a minute later.

Somewhere? That sounds suspiciously like a date.

Would that be a problem?

Not at all. Saturday sounds good.

On Saturday, they met near the station. Taki had chosen a small place for lunch, followed by a walk through a neighborhood with a lovely view of the city. Mitsuha approved of the plan, although she informed him that she had already researched three dessert shops nearby.

“Three?” he asked.

“I don't know how long we'll want to stay at each one.”

“You planned a dessert route?”

“Obviously.”

“I should have known.”

The day passed more quickly than either expected. They tried a dessert they couldn't pronounce, took photographs, and got slightly lost after Taki confidently chose the wrong street. Mitsuha laughed at him for nearly ten minutes.

“You said you knew the way!”

“I knew the general direction.”

“That is not the same thing!”

“We're still having fun, aren't we?”

She tried to look annoyed, but her smile gave her away.

Near sunset, they found a quiet place overlooking the city. The sky was slowly turning gold. Mitsuha stood beside Taki, watching the lights begin to appear in the buildings below.

“Can I ask you something?” she said.

“Of course.”

“Do you still feel that strange familiarity? Like we should have known each other already?”

Taki thought about it. “Sometimes. But it's different now.”

“Different how?”

“Before, it felt like I was missing something. Now I just look forward to seeing you.”

Mitsuha lowered her eyes. A quiet smile appeared on her face.

“I think I feel the same.”

For a while, they watched the sunset without speaking. Taki wondered whether the feeling would ever have an explanation. Perhaps it would. Perhaps it wouldn't. For once, he didn't feel that he needed an answer immediately.

“Mitsuha?”

“Hmm?”

“I'd like to see you again. Not just next weekend. I mean… I'd like to keep spending time with you. Properly.”

She turned toward him. “Are you asking me out?”

“I think I am.”

“Then you should probably ask more clearly.”

Taki took a breath. “Mitsuha Miyamizu, would you like to go on a date with me?”

She pretended to consider it for a moment longer than necessary.

“Yes, Taki Tachibana. I'd like that.”

He smiled. She did too. No memories returned. No mysterious voice explained their connection. The past remained somewhere beyond their reach.

But the future was right there: a dinner they hadn't chosen yet, another place to visit, messages waiting on their phones, and the promise of meeting again.

As they walked toward the station, Mitsuha reached for his hand. Taki looked down, surprised, then gently held it.

“You know,” she said, “when we first met, I was afraid that if I walked away, I'd never see you again.”

“I'm glad you didn't.”

“Me too.”

They reached the ticket gates. Their trains were in different directions, but this time they knew exactly when they would see each other again.

“Tomorrow?” Taki asked.

“Tomorrow,” Mitsuha answered. “And don't be late.”

“I'll try.”

“Taki!”

“I'll be early. I promise.”

She laughed, and he carried that sound with him all the way home.

They might never remember why their paths had crossed, or why the sight of a stranger had once felt like finding something precious. They didn't need to pretend otherwise. They had met, they had chosen to stay, and now they could choose each other again tomorrow.

This time, they would get to know every little thing.
