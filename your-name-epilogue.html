Your Name — A Little More Time

Three small chapters for the story that deserved one more afternoon.

This is an unofficial, non-canonical fan-made continuation written for personal enjoyment. The original film's story and characters belong to their respective creators. This page is separate from Tidewritten's Library and catalogue.
Chapter One

The First Conversation

For a few seconds after they exchanged names, neither of them moved.

The city continued around them. Shoes tapped against the pavement, a train rumbled somewhere beyond the buildings, and the evening light rested on the stairway as if nothing extraordinary had happened there. Yet Taki felt as though the whole world had quietly stepped aside to give them room.

Mitsuha looked down at the name she had written in her phone. Then she looked up again.

“Taki.”

“Yeah?”

“I don't want to forget this time.”

He understood without asking what she meant. He had spent too long carrying the feeling of an unfinished sentence. He took out his phone and showed her the contact he had just saved.

“Then let's make it difficult to forget. I'll message you when I get home.”

She smiled, a little uncertainly. “You'd better.”

They walked down the stairs together. At the bottom, the pavement divided in two directions. Neither seemed particularly eager to choose one.

“Have you eaten?” Taki asked at last.

Mitsuha blinked. “That's your first question?”

“I panicked.”

She laughed. It was a small, surprised laugh, and it loosened something in his chest.

They found a quiet café nearby. At first their conversation stumbled: the weather, the crowded trains, the strange coincidence of meeting on the same staircase. Then Mitsuha asked about the dreams.

“Did you ever dream you were somewhere completely unfamiliar?” she said. “And somehow everyone there knew you?”

Taki set his cup down carefully. “A town surrounded by mountains?”

Her expression changed.

“A lake,” he continued. “A school. A festival.”

For a moment, the café seemed very quiet.

Mitsuha nodded. “It was real, then.”

“I think so.”

They didn't try to explain everything that evening. There were too many memories, too many missing pieces, and too many questions to fit between two cups of tea. Instead, they agreed to meet again the next day.

When they finally stood outside the station, Mitsuha checked her phone twice, making sure his number was still there.

“Tomorrow?” she asked.

“Tomorrow.”

This time, when they went in different directions, neither felt that the other was disappearing.

Chapter Two

The Memories Between Us

The next afternoon, they met at a small park. Taki arrived early. Mitsuha arrived five minutes later and accused him of making her look late.

“You are late,” he said.

“By five minutes. That's practically on time.”

“I'll remember that.”

They found a bench beneath a tree and began comparing the pieces they could still reach. Some memories came easily: the feeling of waking up somewhere unfamiliar, the awkwardness of learning another person's routine, the panic of discovering that a day had passed without knowing what had happened.

Other memories were less clear. They remembered emotions more vividly than details. A promise. A warning. A hand reaching through the fading light. The certainty that someone important was waiting, even when they couldn't remember a face.

Mitsuha told him about Itomori: the lake, the school, the narrow streets, and the people who had filled her days. She spoke about her family and her friends, and about how strange it felt to know that Taki had once walked through her ordinary life.

“You were terrible at my handwriting,” she said.

“Your handwriting was difficult.”

“You wrote like you were in a hurry.”

“I probably was. I had to figure out how to live your life before breakfast.”

She laughed again, then grew thoughtful.

“I used to wonder whether you were a real person or just someone from a dream.”

Taki looked at the path in front of them. “I wondered the same thing. Sometimes I thought I was forgetting something important. I just didn't know what.”

“I know.”

There was no need to make the silence uncomfortable. For once, they could let it stay.

After a while, Taki opened his phone and showed her a photograph of the town as he remembered it. Mitsuha leaned closer, pointing out a street and correcting the angle from which he had taken it.

“You really were there,” she whispered.

“And you were in Tokyo.”

“We were looking at the same sky without knowing it.”

They spent the rest of the afternoon walking through the park, filling in little details and discovering that their memories did not need to be perfect to be shared. Some parts would always remain blurred. That was all right. They could ask each other again whenever they wanted.

At the station, Mitsuha stopped before going through the ticket gate.

“Next time, tell me something about yourself that has nothing to do with all this.”

Taki considered it. “I burn toast.”

“That's not a personality.”

“It's an important warning.”

She shook her head, smiling. “I'll bring breakfast next time, then.”

“I'll look forward to it.”

They said goodbye with the easy promise of another meeting. It felt wonderfully ordinary.

Chapter Three

Tomorrow, Together

A week later, Taki and Mitsuha had developed a routine. They sent messages when they saw something that reminded them of the other, met whenever their schedules allowed, and argued—usually over food—with the seriousness of people who had known each other for years.

On Saturday, Mitsuha arrived carrying a small paper bag.

“Breakfast,” she announced.

Taki looked inside. “You actually remembered?”

“You said you burn toast. I decided society needed protecting.”

He accepted the bag with exaggerated gratitude. They spent the morning wandering through the city, stopping at shops and taking the occasional wrong turn. Mitsuha wanted to see places Taki liked. Taki wanted to show her the little things he had never thought were worth mentioning before.

They bought drinks from a vending machine, watched a train pass, and shared a dessert that was far too large for one person. Nothing remarkable happened. That was precisely why the day felt so precious.

At a quiet viewpoint, the city spread out beneath the afternoon sky. Mitsuha rested her hands on the railing.

“I used to think that if we ever met again, I would have to remember everything,” she said. “Every detail. Every day.”

Taki stood beside her. “Do you still think that?”

She shook her head.

“Not anymore. I want to hear the parts I forgot from you. And I want to tell you the parts you don't know. We can do it slowly.”

“That sounds good.”

“And we should make new memories, too.”

He looked at her. “Like today?”

“Exactly like today.”

The sunlight began to soften. They took a photograph together—not because they were afraid of forgetting, but because they wanted something simple to look back on. In the picture, Taki was halfway through a laugh and Mitsuha was trying not to laugh at him.

She checked the photo and smiled.

“We look happy.”

“We are, aren't we?”

“I think so.”

On the way back, they stopped near the station. Their trains would leave from different platforms, but neither hurried away.

“Taki?”

“Yeah?”

“Can I ask you something?”

“Anything.”

She took a breath. “Would you like to go out with me? Not to compare memories or solve a mystery. Just… a date.”

For once, Taki had no clever answer ready. He smiled instead.

“I was hoping you'd ask.”

Mitsuha looked away, trying and failing to hide her grin. “Then it's a date.”

They chose a day, a time, and a place. They argued over which restaurant to try. Mitsuha insisted that Taki should choose the next dessert; Taki insisted that this was a dangerous responsibility. Their plans were small, ordinary, and entirely theirs.

When the train arrived, they stepped toward their separate platforms. This time, the distance between them was only a few stops and a promise written in both their phones.

Taki turned back once.

“Mitsuha!”

She looked over.

“I'll see you tomorrow.”

Her smile reached him even across the station.

“You'd better.”

And tomorrow came. Then another day, and another after that. They kept talking, kept meeting, and kept learning who the other person was—not as a dream, not as a memory, but as someone standing right there.

They had spent so long trying to find each other. Now they had something just as wonderful to do.

They had time.
