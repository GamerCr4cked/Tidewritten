Chapter One Three Owners and a Tiny World

Yuto Nakamura was seventeen and far more comfortable behind a screen than across a table. In person, he could spend an entire lunch break thinking of something to say. Online, he could manage a server, solve a plugin conflict, and talk until sunrise. The Minecraft community he helped run was small. Its three owners—Yuto, Haru, and Sota—made very little money from it, but they treated every new player like the beginning of something important. Haru was the responsible one, the friend who read documentation and reminded the others to make backups. Sota was chaos with a keyboard. He once set the server's admin password to something so obvious that Haru delivered a fifteen-minute lecture on security. Sota changed the password by adding one digit. Their late-night voice calls were full of bad jokes, server problems, and the kind of laughter that made the clock irrelevant. The community had fifty or sixty regular players, but to the three owners it felt like an entire world they had built with their own hands. Then a new user joined. Her name was Maya. Her profile picture was an anime girl surrounded by flowers, and she asked whether the server offered free hosting for small projects. Yuto answered politely, showed her the options, and helped her set things up. That should have been the end of it. Instead, Maya returned the next day. Then the day after that. She joined the voice calls, learned the regulars' jokes, and began messaging Yuto directly. He discovered that she was funny, patient when he struggled to explain something, and surprisingly good at noticing when he was quieter than usual. Haru noticed too. “You're checking the server every three minutes,” he said one night. “I'm monitoring performance.” “Of course. The server's performance has a flower emoji for a profile picture.” Yuto denied everything. Sota laughed so hard that he muted his microphone. Yuto was not ready to call it a crush. He only knew that the green online dot beside Maya's name had become a small, welcome part of his day.

Chapter Two Screenshots and Promises

Maya and Yuto became close in the way online friendships often do: one conversation at a time, until the hours no longer seemed unusual. They shared screenshots of builds, playlists, and photographs of ordinary things outside their windows. Maya talked about school and family pressure. Yuto told her about the loneliness he rarely admitted to people who knew him offline. He helped her with her server. She helped him name a project he had been struggling to finish. When he worried that the community was growing too slowly, she reminded him that the people who returned every day were the reason it mattered. For a while, everything felt easy. Then Maya began disappearing from conversations. She said she was busy. Her replies became shorter, and she stopped joining the regular voice calls. Yuto tried to give her room, but the sudden change made him anxious. He asked whether something was wrong. “Nothing,” she replied. “Just a lot going on.” He wanted to believe her. He also wished she would tell him when she needed distance rather than leaving him to guess. Haru told him not to build an entire explanation out of a few missed messages. Sota suggested sending a meme, because Sota believed most problems could be improved by a sufficiently stupid picture. Yuto tried both. Neither changed much. Maya eventually returned to the server for a few days, cheerful enough that Yuto hoped the difficult patch had passed. She joined the calls and laughed at Sota's jokes. Then she vanished again, this time for longer. Yuto began keeping track of the little signs: the unanswered message, the empty voice channel, the status that stayed offline. He knew it was not healthy to let a glowing dot determine his mood. Knowing did not make it easy to stop.

Chapter Three A Message from a Stranger

Weeks later, Maya returned with a message that made no sense. She said she had been dealing with a serious medical problem and had undergone surgery. Her memory, she explained, was affected. She could remember some parts of her life but not others. Yuto was frightened for her. He asked whether she was safe and whether she had support around her. She answered briefly, then asked a question that made his chest tighten. “I'm sorry. Who are you?” He thought it was a joke. He reminded her of the server, the late-night calls, the jokes they had shared. She responded politely, as though he were a stranger telling her a story about someone else. Haru and Sota tried to help. They sent messages too, explaining who they were and what the friendship had meant. Maya said she did not remember them. Yuto wanted to believe the explanation. Memory problems after medical treatment can be complicated, and he had no way to know what Maya could or could not recall. But questions kept returning: she recognised the server, knew how to use her account, and seemed familiar with parts of the community while not recognising the people who had been closest to her. He wondered whether she had forgotten them, whether she was overwhelmed, or whether she wanted to leave and did not know how to say it. He could not answer any of those questions for her. He sent one final message, not asking her to prove anything and not demanding a return. “I hope you're safe. You don't have to reply. I just wanted you to know that our friendship mattered to me.” The message stayed unread.

Chapter Four The Green Dot

The server kept running, but it no longer felt the same. Yuto stopped checking whether Maya was online quite so often. He still caught himself doing it, especially late at night, when the voice channel was quiet and the familiar names were missing. Eventually, he sold the server and handed the community over to someone who wanted to manage it. Haru and Sota protested. They reminded him how much work he had put into building it. “I know,” Yuto said. “I just don't feel like it's mine anymore.” He did not sell it because the friendship had been meaningless. He sold it because every familiar channel had become a place where he waited for someone who might never return. Haru and Sota stayed. They continued to call, play games, and argue about plugins. They let Yuto be quiet without treating his silence as a problem to solve. Slowly, he began to enjoy the online world again without needing it to bring Maya back. Years later, he still did not know whether her memory had truly changed, whether she had chosen distance, or whether both things had happened in ways he could never understand. He refused to turn uncertainty into an accusation. Her reasons belonged to her, even if he had been hurt by the way things ended. He learned to stop asking the green dot for answers.

Chapter Five Aap Kaun?

One night, long after the server was gone, Yuto opened an old folder of screenshots. There were silly builds, late-night jokes, and a picture of the three owners standing together in a blocky Minecraft world. He laughed at one of Sota's messages. Then he found a screenshot of Maya's first greeting. It was only a hello. He sat with the memory for a while. The friendship had mattered. The laughter had happened. The fact that it ended without clarity did not make those moments false. Yuto closed the folder and messaged Haru and Sota. “Anyone free for a call?” Sota joined immediately and began complaining about a game update. Haru arrived a minute later, asking whether Yuto had remembered to eat. Their familiar voices filled the room. Yuto found himself laughing. He was still quieter than most people. He still preferred a screen to a crowded room. But he was learning that one person's disappearance did not have to turn every other connection into a risk. There would always be a question he could not answer. Maybe Maya had forgotten. Maybe she had wanted to leave. Maybe the truth was more complicated than either possibility. He could not make her explain. He could choose what to carry forward. Some people go offline for a night and return the next morning. Some leave a server without saying goodbye. And some return with a familiar username but a distance that no message can cross. Yuto could not change how the story ended. He could remember that he had once been happy in that little digital world—and build a life that held more than waiting. The green dot went dark. The world kept moving. And, eventually, so did he.
