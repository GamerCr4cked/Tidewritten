Chapter One Eight Hundred Kilometres

Summer began with calls that lasted until midnight. Mio showed Akito the view from her family's balcony; Akito sent photographs of the evening sky above his hometown. They traded jokes, voice messages, and ordinary updates that made the distance feel manageable.

Then the calls grew shorter.

Mio was busy with family visits, she said. She had errands, old friends to see, and days when she was too tired to talk. Akito believed her. Trust, to him, meant not treating every delay as evidence.

But a few weeks later, he saw a photograph online. Mio stood with a group of old friends. A young man named Ryo stood close beside her, one hand resting on the back of her chair.

The photograph alone meant nothing. Akito knew that. He asked about the outing, trying to sound casual.

Mio said it had been fun, then told him not to overthink it.

The phrase hurt more than he expected. It made a reasonable question sound like a flaw.

When they returned to Seihoku for the second year, Akito hoped the distance would disappear with the train ride. Mio hugged him at the dormitory entrance, and for a few minutes he believed everything was fine.

Then her phone rang.

She glanced at the screen and stepped outside to answer. Ten minutes later, she returned with a smile that looked carefully assembled.

“Who was it?” Akito asked.

“An old friend.”

“Ryo?”

Her expression changed.

“Why are you making this an interrogation?”

“I'm asking because I feel like something's different.”

“Nothing is different, Akito. Please don't start.”

He apologised, even though he was no longer sure what he was apologising for.

That evening, he sat in his room and watched their message thread. He could still see the early summer messages, full of little details and affection. The words were still there. The ease behind them was not.

Chapter Two The Third Person

Ryo's name appeared more often. A comment under Mio's photographs. A call she could not answer while Akito was speaking. A plan that changed at the last minute.

Akito did not want to police her friendships. He did not want to become someone who demanded screenshots or treated a relationship like a security system. He wanted honesty, and he wanted to believe that asking for it would not make him unreasonable.

One evening, he saw Mio and Ryo leaving a café near the station. They were not holding hands. They were simply walking close together, laughing at something on Ryo's phone.

Akito waited until they were alone before asking her about it.

Mio went quiet. Then she admitted that Ryo had confessed he liked her. She said she had been confused, that she had enjoyed the attention, and that she had not known how to explain it to Akito.

“Did something happen between you?” he asked.

She looked away for too long.

The answer arrived in fragments: a kiss, a decision she regretted, messages she had hidden because she was afraid of losing him. There were no excuses that could make the facts harmless.

Akito listened until she finished.

“I should have told you,” Mio whispered.

“Yes.”

“I was scared.”

“I understand being scared.” His voice sounded distant even to himself. “But you let me keep trusting a version of things that wasn't true.”

Mio began to cry. Akito wanted to comfort her; the instinct was immediate and painful. He still cared about her. That was precisely why the truth hurt.

He told her he needed space.

For once, she did not ask him to calm down or promise that it meant nothing. She only nodded.

They walked away in opposite directions.

That night, Akito did not block her, insult her, or send a final speech. He put his phone away and sat with the reality that love could remain even after trust had been damaged beyond what he could repair that day.

Chapter Three The Shape of Silence

The next weeks were quieter than Akito had imagined possible.

He studied in the library, took photographs around campus, and spent more time with Kenta. He started a small photography series: empty benches, corridors after class, rain on windows, the places people passed through without noticing.

He called it *Colours of Silence*.

The photographs found an audience online. People praised their atmosphere and asked what each image meant. Akito rarely answered. He was still learning how to explain the things he felt.

Mio, meanwhile, stopped trying to force a conversation. She sent one apology, then another a week later. Akito read them but did not reply. He was not trying to punish her. He simply did not have an answer that would be honest.

She wrote messages in a notebook and never sent them. She wanted to explain the confusion, the fear, the selfishness that had led her to hide the truth. But the more she wrote, the clearer it became that an explanation could not undo what she had chosen.

At college, they sometimes passed in the corridor. At first, both looked away. Later, Akito managed a small nod. Mio returned it, her eyes full of words she did not ask him to hear.

They had once been the pair everyone expected to find together. Now their friends learned not to ask about the other one.

The hardest part was not that Akito could no longer speak to Mio. It was that he still noticed things she would have liked: a ridiculous poster, a new drink at the vending machine, a patch of sunlight on the stairs.

Care did not switch off when a relationship ended. It lingered, without knowing where to go.

Chapter Four No More Excuses

Months passed. Akito began to feel steady again, though not untouched. He discovered that a good day did not mean he had forgotten Mio, and a difficult day did not mean he had made the wrong choice by stepping away.

Mio stopped looking for a way to make him answer. She spoke honestly to Yuna about what she had done. She did not blame Ryo, distance, loneliness, or Akito's questions. Those things had existed; the choices had still been hers.

“I wanted to avoid one painful conversation,” she told Yuna. “So I created a hundred worse ones.”

She ended contact with Ryo and began taking responsibility for the damage she had caused. It did not earn her forgiveness. It was simply the right thing to do.

Near the end of the semester, Akito and Mio met unexpectedly outside the library. They stood beneath the same overhang while rain tapped against the pavement.

“I'm not going to ask you to forgive me,” Mio said. “I just want you to know I understand why you left.”

Akito looked at her. She seemed tired, but there was no attempt to make him responsible for comforting her.

“Thank you for saying that,” he replied.

It was not reconciliation. It was not a promise.

For the first time in months, the silence between them felt less like a weapon and more like a boundary neither of them needed to break.

They parted politely.

Akito walked home in the rain, carrying the ache of what had happened and the quiet relief of no longer arguing with his own memory.

Chapter Five Meet Again

Summer came again. This time, neither of them called.

Akito opened the notebook where he had once written about the first day he met Mio. The early pages were full of jokes, observations, and the ordinary details that had made their friendship feel effortless.

He added one final entry:

*Some people show you beautiful things and painful things. Both become part of your life. I don't have to pretend the beautiful part was false just because the painful part happened.*

He closed the notebook.

Mio wrote a similar sentence in her own diary, though she did not know what Akito had written. She stopped asking mutual friends whether he was doing well. She hoped he was. She accepted that hope did not entitle her to a place in his life.

When the new academic year began, they passed each other in a corridor. Akito gave her a small, genuine nod. Mio returned it.

They kept walking.

It was not the ending either had once imagined. There was no final argument, no perfect apology, no instant return to the people they had been. Their relationship had ended, and the consequences were real.

Still, the first year had happened. The laughter had been real. The rain had been real. The affection they had shared was not erased by what came after.

Akito turned toward the library, camera in his bag. Mio headed for class, a notebook tucked beneath her arm.

Neither knew whether they would ever meet again in a way that mattered.

For now, they were learning to live without an answer.

*Phir milenge* had once meant a promise that the distance would end soon.

Now it meant something smaller and more honest: life was long, people changed, and no one could demand a reunion from the future.

The story was not finished. But this part of it was.

Neither of them could return to the relationship they had before the truth came out. If they ever met again, it would have to be as the people they had become—not as the people who still wished the past could be undone.
