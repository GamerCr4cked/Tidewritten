Chapter One The Empty Seat

Akito Mori had a system for surviving college: arrive early, choose a seat near the back, put in his earphones, and avoid unnecessary conversation. It had worked for eighteen years. Then, on orientation day at Seihoku College, a girl arrived late with a half-spilled iced coffee and the expression of someone who had already lost an argument with the morning. Every seat in the auditorium was taken except the one beside Akito. “Is this seat free?” she whispered. He nodded. “Great. If I fall asleep, please wake me before the important part.” “Do you know what the important part is?” “No. That's why I need you.” Her name was Mio Tachibana. She whispered commentary through the dean's speech, made a serious announcement about the terrible auditorium air-conditioning, and somehow turned a two-hour orientation into something Akito could remember. When it ended, he reached for his earphones. “I'm getting lunch,” Mio said. “You can come if you want.” His usual answer would have been no. He had a room to unpack and a schedule to organise. He was perfectly capable of eating alone. Instead, he heard himself say, “All right.” Mio smiled as if she'd expected it. The cafeteria was noisy, the curry was average, and Mio talked enough for both of them. She asked where he came from, what he studied, and whether he always looked as if he were preparing to leave a room. “I don't.” “You absolutely do.” Akito laughed before he could stop himself. He didn't know it yet, but that laugh would become one of the small things he remembered when everything between them grew complicated.

Chapter Two A Habit Called Mio

Mio returned the next day. And the day after that. By the end of the first week, the seat beside Akito had become unofficially hers. If he arrived first, he left his bag on the neighbouring chair. If she arrived first, she waved him over with the confidence of someone reserving a table in her own house. Their friendship grew by repetition. Lunch became a daily ritual. They studied together, complained about lecturers, and took the long path back from the library because Mio insisted the vending machines near the garden had better drinks. She tried everything the machine offered. Akito drank black coffee. “You order the same thing every time,” she said. “Because I know I like it.” “How do you know you don't like the others?” “I have eyes.” “That's not how taste works, Professor Mori.” At night, messages followed: a photograph of her disastrous instant noodles, a question about an assignment, a meme sent at midnight with no explanation. Akito began checking his phone without thinking. His roommate, Kenta, noticed. “You've become very available lately.” “I answer messages.” “You used to treat messages like letters from the tax office.” Akito threw a pillow at him. He called it a habit. It was a convenient word. Habits did not require him to ask why Mio's laughter could improve a bad day, or why a quiet cafeteria felt strangely empty when she was absent. He didn't yet understand that a routine could become a person-shaped place in your life.

Chapter Three Best Friends, Apparently

By the end of the first semester, everyone at Seihoku knew Akito and Mio as a pair. They were not dating. They had not discussed dating. They had, however, developed the ability to communicate entire conversations through a glance across a lecture hall. When Mio forgot her student card, Akito waited at the gate. When Akito skipped lunch to finish a report, Mio arrived with an extra rice ball and an expression that discouraged debate. “You can't live on coffee,” she told him. “I can try.” “You can also fail.” Their friends were less cautious about the obvious. Kenta asked Akito whether he planned to marry Mio or simply reserve the seat beside him for the next four years. Mio's friend Yuna was even worse. “You two are either secretly together or the world's slowest people.” “We're friends,” Mio replied. She said it lightly. Akito noticed that she looked at him for a second after saying it. That night, he replayed the moment while staring at his ceiling. He had started noticing details he had no reason to collect: the way Mio tucked her hair behind one ear when concentrating, the way she went quiet when something genuinely worried her, the way she always checked whether he had eaten. The realisation came slowly and then all at once. He liked her. Not as a funny classmate. Not as a convenient routine. He liked the person who had found the empty seat beside him and made his carefully organised days feel more alive. The trouble was that friendship had become precious. He was frightened of changing it by naming what he felt.

Chapter Four The Space Between Us

Spring arrived with cherry blossoms along the campus path. Mio dragged Akito outside after a lecture, declaring that a person who spent every sunny afternoon indoors deserved to be reported. They sat beneath a flowering tree, sharing a packet of convenience-store sweets. “Can I ask you something?” Mio said. “Depends.” “Do you ever think about what happens after college?” “Jobs. Rent. Probably more coffee.” “I'm serious.” “So am I. Coffee is expensive.” She nudged his shoulder, but she didn't laugh for long. She told him she was afraid that everyone would scatter after graduation, that friendships built around shared schedules might disappear once the schedules changed. Akito wanted to tell her that he didn't want to lose her. The words rose, reached his throat, and stopped. “People can make an effort,” he said instead. Mio looked at him. “Would you?” “Yes.” It was not a confession. It was not enough to explain what he felt. But her expression softened, and she leaned back against the tree. “Good. I would too.” The moment stayed with him for weeks. He tried to be more attentive without becoming strange. He walked her to the station when their classes ended together. He remembered the drink she liked. He sent messages first sometimes, even when he had nothing particularly clever to say. Mio noticed the changes. Once, when he handed her a warm canned drink on a cold evening, she looked at it and then at him. “You're getting thoughtful.” “Don't spread rumours.” “Too late. I'm telling everyone.” She smiled, but there was something searching in her eyes. For the first time, Akito wondered whether she was waiting for him to say something too.

Chapter Five Say It Before the Train

The first year ended under a soft summer rain. Exams were over, suitcases appeared in the dormitory hall, and students began planning their trips home. Mio was leaving the next morning. Her family lived several prefectures away, and the break would keep them apart for weeks. They met outside the station after an afternoon of packing. The rain had eased to a fine mist. Mio held a clear umbrella; Akito carried one he had borrowed from Kenta. “You look like you're about to give a speech,” she said. “I've been told I should talk more.” “By whom?” “Everyone.” “Wise people.” He laughed, then fell quiet. The train announcement sounded from the platform. “Mio, I like you.” The words were not elegant. They came out quickly, as if he were afraid his courage might expire. Mio stopped smiling—not because she was unhappy, but because she was trying to understand whether she had heard him correctly. “I like you,” he repeated. “As more than a friend. You don't have to answer now. I just didn't want you to leave without knowing.” For a few seconds, rain whispered against the umbrella. Then Mio looked down, smiling so softly that he almost missed it. “You really took your time, Akito.” His heart stumbled. “Is that a bad thing?” “No.” She looked up. “It's a very good thing.” She told him she liked him too. She had been afraid to say it first, afraid of being the only one who had imagined something more in their friendship. The train arrived. They did not suddenly become different people. They were still awkward, still teasing, still unsure how a relationship was supposed to begin. But when Mio boarded, she turned at the door. “Call me tonight.” “I will.” “And tomorrow.” “All right.” “And don't pretend you forgot.” “I won't.” The doors closed. The train carried her away, and Akito stood on the platform with rain on his sleeves and a smile he couldn't hide.

Chapter Six Phir Milenge

Their relationship began without an announcement. No dramatic post. No declaration in the cafeteria. Their friends figured it out when Mio returned from the summer break and immediately took the seat beside Akito, then reached for his hand under the table as if it were the most natural thing in the world. Kenta nearly choked on his tea. “You were dating this whole time?” “Not the whole time,” Akito said. Mio tilted her head. “Depends when you started counting.” Their days became warmer, but not perfect. They still disagreed about music, study schedules, and whether Akito's coffee counted as a proper drink. They learned to apologise instead of turning every misunderstanding into a contest. One afternoon, rain trapped them in the cafeteria. Mio watched the water slide down the window. “Want to go outside?” she asked. “It's raining.” “Excellent observation.” Five minutes later, they were in the courtyard, laughing beneath the open sky. Mio spun once with her arms out, and Akito watched her with the helpless affection of someone who had finally stopped pretending he was unaffected. She came back to him, rain on her hair and laughter in her eyes. “Still glad you came to lunch that first day?” “Yes,” he said. “Very.” She rested her forehead lightly against his. They stayed like that for a moment, with the rain falling around them and the campus blurred into silver. When the first year ended, summer would take them to different cities again. They knew it would be difficult. They also knew they wanted to try. At the station, Mio waved as her train arrived. “See you soon,” she said. Akito smiled. “Phir milenge.” They would meet again. Neither of them knew yet how much those words would come to mean.
