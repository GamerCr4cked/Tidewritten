Chapter One A Small Server, a Big World

GamerCr4cked knew how to fix a broken plugin faster than he knew how to explain what he was feeling.

That was one reason the internet had become comfortable. In a voice call, he could talk about Minecraft for hours—permissions, hosting, builds, the next update, a new idea that probably needed more work than he had time for. In person, even an ordinary conversation could leave him searching for the right words.

The server was small, held together by late nights, borrowed patience, and the three people who ran it. GamerCr4cked handled the technical mess. KingOP was the steady one when everyone else got carried away. Alone could turn a chaotic idea into a plan—and then make fun of the plan while helping build it.

They argued over plugins, forgot to announce maintenance, and occasionally spent longer choosing a server icon than solving an actual problem. But when a new player joined, they tried to make the place feel welcoming. It was not a huge network. It was theirs.

Bunny arrived through a question about setting up a small project. GamerCr4cked answered with a practical explanation, expecting the conversation to end there.

It did not.

Bunny came back the next evening. Then she joined a group call, listened to KingOP and Alone argue about something completely unimportant, and eventually started joining often enough that her name felt like part of the room.

She was funny without trying to dominate every conversation. She could tell when GamerCr4cked had gone quiet, and she did not always demand that he explain why. Sometimes she simply sent him a message about a build or a video and let the conversation find its own pace.

KingOP noticed the change before GamerCr4cked admitted there was one.

“You checked the member list again,” KingOP said during a call.

“I'm checking if anyone needs help.”

“Of course.”

Alone made a thoughtful noise. “The server has never been monitored this romantically.”

“Both of you are getting muted,” GamerCr4cked said.

They laughed. Bunny, who had heard everything, sent him a single laughing emoji.

It was a small thing. It still made his whole evening better.

He did not have a name for what he felt, and he did not need one yet. He only knew that when Bunny appeared online, the server felt a little more alive.

Chapter Two Messages After Midnight

Their friendship grew through ordinary things: screenshots of unfinished builds, clips that made no sense without context, complaints about school, family expectations, and the frustrating feeling that everyone expected them to know what they were doing with their lives.

Some nights they talked about Minecraft. Other nights they barely mentioned it.

Bunny asked about a project GamerCr4cked had been postponing. He helped her figure out a technical problem she had been stuck on. They sent each other music, shared jokes that became funnier through repetition, and learned each other's patterns: who replied immediately, who disappeared during a busy day, who said “I'm fine” when they clearly needed a little space.

GamerCr4cked found himself telling Bunny things he usually kept to himself. She listened without turning every problem into a lecture. In return, he tried to be there when she had a difficult day, even when he did not know the perfect thing to say.

The friendship began to feel important in a way that made him careful. He did not want to put pressure on it by assuming it meant the same thing to both of them.

For a while, there was no reason to worry.

Then Bunny's replies became less frequent.

At first it was understandable. She said she was busy. She missed a call, then another. Messages that once became conversations now ended after a sentence or two. When GamerCr4cked asked if something was wrong, she said she had a lot going on.

He accepted the answer, but the uncertainty stayed.

KingOP told him not to build a whole story from a few missed messages. Alone suggested that he ask once, clearly, then give Bunny room to respond. Their advice was sensible. It was also hard to follow when every quiet evening made him want to check whether she had come online.

He sent a funny clip. No reply.

He asked if she wanted to join the server later. She said maybe, then did not appear.

A few days later, Bunny returned to the group call and laughed at one of Alone's terrible jokes. GamerCr4cked felt relief arrive too quickly, as if a few minutes of normal conversation could settle every question he had been carrying.

It could not.

After that call, the silence returned.

He knew a green online dot was not a promise. He knew people had lives beyond a screen, and that nobody owed him constant access to their time. What hurt was not simply that Bunny was busy. It was that he no longer understood where he stood with someone who had once made him feel understood.

He tried to give her space without pretending it did not hurt.

Chapter Three “Who Are You?”

Weeks passed before Bunny returned with an explanation GamerCr4cked had not expected.

She told him she had been dealing with a serious medical problem and had undergone surgery. Her memory, she said, had been affected. She could recall some parts of her life but not others.

His first reaction was worry. He asked if she was safe and whether she had people around her who could support her. He did not want to interrogate someone who might already be dealing with something frightening.

Bunny replied briefly. Then she asked a question.

“Sorry… who are you?”

For a moment, GamerCr4cked thought she was joking. He reminded her of the Minecraft server, the late-night calls, the screenshots, the conversations that had stretched past midnight. He tried to keep the message gentle, as if the right words might help her recognise him.

Her answer remained distant. She said she did not remember him.

KingOP and Alone reached out too. They explained who they were and how they knew her. Bunny said she did not remember them either.

GamerCr4cked wanted to believe her explanation. He also could not make himself stop wondering. She seemed to recognise parts of the online community, but not the people who had been closest to her. He had no access to her medical reality, no way to measure what she remembered, and no right to decide what her experience must be.

There were possibilities he could not separate. Perhaps her memory really had changed. Perhaps she felt overwhelmed and wanted distance. Perhaps the truth was more complicated than either explanation. He could not know, and guessing harder would not turn uncertainty into fact.

For several days, he wrote messages and deleted them. He wanted to ask why she had returned only to treat him like a stranger. He wanted her to understand how much the silence had hurt. He also knew that demanding an explanation would not create one.

Finally, he sent a short message.

“I hope you're safe and getting support. You don't have to reply. I just wanted you to know that our friendship mattered to me.”

The message stayed unread.

He did not send another.

Chapter Four A Server Full of Echoes

The server kept running, but GamerCr4cked no longer experienced it the same way.

A familiar channel could remind him of a joke Bunny had made. A half-finished build could bring back a conversation he could no longer continue. Sometimes he opened the member list without thinking, then closed it as soon as he realised what he was doing.

KingOP and Alone stayed close. They did not insist that he get over it, and they did not encourage him to turn Bunny into a villain. They played games, talked about projects, and let him be quiet when he had nothing to say.

Eventually, GamerCr4cked admitted that he no longer wanted to manage the server. He arranged for someone else to take it over, made sure the community knew what was changing, and handed over the work he had spent so long maintaining.

KingOP was unhappy about it. Alone asked if he was sure.

“I'm sure about needing a break,” GamerCr4cked said. “I don't know if I'll feel this way forever. But right now, every channel feels like I'm waiting for something.”

He did not abandon the server out of anger. The place had been real to him. The friendships, the laughter, the work they had done together—all of it had mattered. But he could not keep living inside the expectation that Bunny might return and make everything feel like it used to.

The months after that were not dramatic. There was no sudden revelation and no perfect closure. He still thought about her sometimes. He still wondered whether she remembered them, whether she was okay, and whether the explanation she gave was the whole story.

He learned that caring about someone did not mean he could solve their silence. He could wish Bunny well without chasing her for an answer. He could admit that he had been hurt without deciding that he knew her intentions.

Most importantly, he could let the other friendships in his life remain open.

Chapter Five The People Who Stayed

One evening, long after the server had changed hands, GamerCr4cked found a folder of old screenshots.

There were blocky builds, ridiculous chat messages, a picture of the three owners standing beside a half-finished spawn, and a screenshot of Bunny's first question in the support channel.

It was an ordinary hello. He had no idea how much would grow from it.

He scrolled through the folder without trying to turn every memory into a clue. The friendship had mattered. The laughter had happened. Its ending did not erase the time when it had made his days brighter.

He messaged KingOP and Alone.

“Anyone free for a call?”

Alone joined first, immediately complaining about something that had gone wrong with a game. KingOP arrived a minute later and began arguing about a plugin none of them even used anymore. GamerCr4cked laughed before he could stop himself.

The familiar sound of their call settled the room.

He was still more comfortable behind a screen than in a crowded place. He still sometimes struggled to explain what he felt. But he was learning that one confusing ending did not have to make every future connection feel unsafe.

He would probably never know exactly what had happened with Bunny. He could respect the limits of what she had shared, accept that he could not verify the rest, and stop asking himself to solve a question that belonged to someone else's life.

He hoped she was okay. He hoped the good parts of their friendship had meant something to her too. He could hold those hopes without putting his life on pause.

The old server was gone from his control, but the world had not ended with it. There were still projects to build, games to play, and people who answered when he called.

Sometimes people went offline and came back the next morning. Sometimes they returned with a familiar username and a distance no message could cross. Sometimes a friendship ended without a clear goodbye.

GamerCr4cked could not choose which kind of ending he had been given.

He could choose what he did next.

He closed the screenshot folder, joined the call, and listened as KingOP and Alone argued about absolutely nothing.

For the first time in a long while, he was not waiting for a notification.

He was already where he wanted to be.
