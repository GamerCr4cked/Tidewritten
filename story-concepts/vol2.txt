# THE GIRL WHO HATED EVERYONE EXCEPT THE BOY WHO WOULDN'T CHASE HER
## Volume Two: The Things We Never Said

*A story about the secrets people keep to protect each other, the lies that grow in the silence, and the terrible distance between loving someone and knowing them.*

---

# PROLOGUE
## The Girl Who Knew His Old Name

The coffee had gone cold.

Hanako Shimizu stared at the can on the bench beside her, watching a thin ribbon of condensation slide down its side.

She had bought it out of habit.

Black coffee. No sugar.

GC's usual choice.

It had taken her three seconds to pick it up from the vending machine. Less than one second to remember that he wasn't there.

She hated that.

She hated how the hand could remember something the heart was trying to forget.

Across the station platform, a train announcement echoed overhead. People hurried past her, carrying bags, talking on phones, living ordinary lives that seemed almost offensively uncomplicated.

Hanako looked at her phone.

No new messages.

The last conversation remained open on the screen.

**Hanako:** Are you coming tomorrow?

**GC:** I don't think I should.

**Hanako:** You don't think, or you don't want to?

**GC:** Hanako—

**Hanako:** Forget it.

That had been four days ago.

She had reread the conversation so many times that she could remember every punctuation mark.

A shadow fell across the bench.

"You're still buying his coffee?"

Hanako looked up.

A girl stood before her, holding a folded umbrella.

She was about Hanako's age, with dark hair tied loosely at the back of her head and a calm expression that seemed strangely out of place in the busy station. Her uniform was unfamiliar. A small silver charm hung from her bag.

Hanako didn't recognize her.

The girl looked at the can.

Then at Hanako.

"You should stop doing that."

Hanako's fingers tightened around her phone.

"Excuse me?"

The girl sat at the other end of the bench without asking permission.

"He doesn't drink that brand anymore."

Hanako went still.

"What did you say?"

The girl turned toward her.

For the first time, her composure faltered.

It wasn't fear.

It was something closer to regret.

"Sorry," she murmured. "I shouldn't have said that."

Hanako stood.

"Who are you?"

The girl looked down at her hands.

"My name is Aoi."

"How do you know GC?"

Aoi didn't answer immediately.

Then she said something that made the station seem to fall silent around them.

"I knew him before he started calling himself GC."

Hanako felt the cold coffee can press against her palm.

Before he started calling himself GC?

She had known him for months.

She knew his favorite drink, his habits, the way he lowered his voice when he was tired, and the way he sometimes smiled when he thought nobody was watching.

She had believed she was slowly learning the parts of him he had hidden from everyone else.

Apparently, there had been a whole life before her.

Aoi stood and adjusted the strap of her bag.

"If you still care about him," she said, "don't ask him about me yet."

Hanako stared at her.

"Why?"

Aoi's expression changed.

For a moment, she looked less like a mysterious stranger and more like someone standing outside a room she desperately wanted to enter.

"Because," she whispered, "I don't know what he'll tell you."

Then she walked away.

Hanako remained beside the bench.

The coffee was cold.

Her phone was silent.

And for the first time since GC had asked her to trust him, she wondered whether he had ever told her the whole truth.

# PART I
## THE DAYS WE THOUGHT WOULD LAST

# CHAPTER 1
## A Relationship Is Not a Magical Cure

Being GC's girlfriend was surprisingly ordinary.

Hanako had expected it to be more complicated.

Not because she believed relationships were easy, but because GC had a remarkable ability to turn simple situations into confusing ones.

For example, he had spent eleven minutes deciding whether he was allowed to hold her hand in public.

They were walking home from school when Hanako finally lost patience.

"You're doing it again."

GC glanced at her.

"Doing what?"

"Looking at my hand."

"I was looking at the road."

"The road is on the other side."

He looked ahead.

"You're right."

"Don't agree with me like that!"

"I thought you wanted me to admit when I was wrong."

"I do. Just not in that tone."

"What tone?"

"That one!"

GC smiled.

Hanako looked away before he could notice her smiling, too.

Then she reached over and took his hand herself.

His fingers closed around hers.

Neither spoke for a while.

It was a small thing.

A perfectly ordinary thing.

And perhaps that was why it mattered.

For most of her life, Hanako had believed that affection should announce itself. People who cared should say so. People who wanted something should ask for it. If someone had a problem, they should explain it rather than expect everyone else to solve a puzzle.

GC had taught her that people were rarely so straightforward.

She had taught him that silence could hurt even when it was meant kindly.

They were still learning.

At lunch, Hiro noticed their joined hands and nearly choked on his drink.

"Oh, this is disgusting."

Hanako released GC's hand immediately.

"What is?"

"Your happiness. It's making the entire classroom smell like a romance novel."

"Would you like me to help you leave?"

"Threats already? I give this relationship three weeks."

GC considered it.

"That would take us to the middle of next month."

Hiro stared at him.

"You just accepted my breakup prediction as a scheduling problem."

"I was clarifying the timeline."

"I hate both of you."

Hanako smirked.

"No, you don't."

Hiro pointed at her.

"Don't steal his lines. That's how it starts."

GC laughed.

Hanako looked at him.

She liked that sound.

Not because it was rare anymore, but because it was becoming familiar.

And familiarity, she was discovering, was a dangerous thing to love.

It gave ordinary moments the power to become memories.

That afternoon, GC walked her to the station.

Before they separated, he looked at her.

"Can I ask you something?"

"Depends."

"Would you be upset if I had to cancel Saturday?"

Hanako's expression changed.

"Why?"

"I have a medical appointment. It was scheduled a while ago, but they changed the time."

She waited for the old answer.

*It's nothing.*

*Don't worry.*

*I'll be fine.*

Instead, he continued.

"I don't know how long it'll take. I might be tired afterward."

Hanako felt something soften inside her.

"Then we'll do something another day."

"You're not angry?"

"Should I be?"

"I don't know."

"Then learn to find out before assuming the worst."

He smiled faintly.

"Right."

She hesitated, then squeezed his hand.

"Tell me how it goes."

"I will."

"And don't skip it just because you think it'll inconvenience me."

"I won't."

She watched him leave.

For once, she believed him.

That evening, a message arrived.

**GC:** Appointment moved again. Saturday should be okay.

**Hanako:** You don't have to force it.

**GC:** I want to go.

She stared at those three words longer than she wanted to admit.

Then she replied.

**Hanako:** Fine. But you're buying me lunch.

**GC:** I thought you were choosing the place.

**Hanako:** I'm choosing the place. You're paying.

**GC:** That seems legally questionable.

**Hanako:** Take it up with the authorities.

**GC:** Hiro's authorities?

**Hanako:** Exactly.

She set the phone down.

For a few minutes, she simply sat there, feeling happy.

Not because everything was perfect.

Because things were imperfect, and they were learning how to handle them together.

She had no idea how much she would miss that feeling.

# CHAPTER 2
## The Girl at the Station

Aoi appeared on a Wednesday.

Hanako recognized her immediately.

The dark hair. The silver charm. The calm eyes.

She was standing outside the school gates, speaking to a teacher.

When the teacher left, Aoi looked toward the building.

Their eyes met.

Aoi gave Hanako a small nod.

Hanako didn't return it.

She had spent the last three days trying to convince herself that the encounter at the station had been unimportant.

Now the girl was standing outside her school.

That seemed rather deliberate.

At lunch, Hanako mentioned it to GC.

"Do you know a girl named Aoi?"

His chopsticks stopped halfway to his mouth.

It lasted less than a second.

Hanako noticed anyway.

"Why?" he asked.

"She was outside the school this morning."

GC set his chopsticks down.

"What did she say to you?"

The question came too quickly.

Hanako's stomach tightened.

"So you do know her."

He looked at her.

"Yes."

"From where?"

A pause.

"Before I came here."

"That isn't an answer."

"I know."

He rubbed his thumb against the edge of his lunch container.

"I need to talk to her first."

Hanako stared at him.

"Why?"

"Because there's something I need to understand."

"About what?"

"Hanako."

She hated the way he said her name when he wanted her to stop asking questions.

Not because it was harsh.

Because it was gentle.

As though he were already apologizing for something he hadn't explained.

"Fine," she said.

GC looked relieved.

That made it worse.

She stood, picked up her lunch, and walked away.

He didn't follow.

For the rest of the afternoon, she told herself she was being reasonable.

People were allowed to have friends from their past. People were allowed to keep private things private.

She didn't need to know every detail of GC's life.

She had told him that herself.

But privacy was one thing.

The expression on his face when she mentioned Aoi was another.

That evening, GC sent her a message.

**GC:** I'm sorry about lunch.

Hanako stared at it.

**Hanako:** For what?

**GC:** For making you feel shut out.

She typed three different replies and deleted them all.

Finally, she sent:

**Hanako:** Are you going to tell me what this is about?

His response took several minutes.

**GC:** Yes. I promise.

Hanako let out a breath.

Then another message arrived.

**GC:** Just not yet.

She stared at the screen.

A promise with a condition.

A door that would open later.

A truth that belonged to him until he decided she was allowed to see it.

She placed the phone face down.

For the first time in their relationship, she wished he had never made a promise at all.

---

# CHAPTER 3
## The Boy Who Remembered Too Much

Aoi approached GC after school.

He was standing near the bicycle racks, checking a message from his mother.

"You're difficult to find."

He looked up.

"You could have contacted me."

"I did."

"I changed my number."

"I noticed."

Aoi looked at him for a long moment.

"You really did it."

GC put his phone away.

"Yes."

"New school. New friends. New name."

"My name hasn't changed."

"You know what I mean."

He did.

That was the problem.

Aoi folded her arms.

"Does she know?"

GC's expression tightened.

"No."

"How long have you been together?"

"Why does that matter?"

"Because if you're serious about her, she deserves to know what happened."

GC looked away.

"I know."

"Then tell her."

"It's not that simple."

Aoi's voice sharpened.

"It never is with you."

He flinched.

She noticed and immediately looked away.

"I'm sorry."

Neither spoke for a while.

Aoi reached into her bag and pulled out a small envelope.

"I found this while cleaning."

GC stared at it.

The envelope was old. The corners had softened, and the handwriting on the front was faded.

His old name was written across it.

Not GC.

The name he had used before transferring schools.

The name he had stopped answering to after the hospital stay that changed more than his medical records.

He didn't take the envelope.

"Where did you get that?"

"Your mother kept some of your things. She asked me to return anything that belonged to you."

GC's face drained of color.

"My mother gave you this?"

"No. I found it in the box she asked me to sort."

He looked at the envelope again.

"Did you read it?"

Aoi hesitated.

"Part of it."

"Why?"

"Because I saw the date."

GC closed his eyes briefly.

"Of course you did."

Aoi's expression softened.

"I didn't come here to hurt you."

"Then why did you come?"

She looked at him.

"Because you left without telling me whether you were going to come back."

The words hung between them.

GC's face changed.

For a moment, the schoolyard disappeared. He was no longer a seventeen-year-old boy standing beside a bicycle rack.

He was a younger version of himself, sitting in a hospital room, staring at a phone he hadn't had the courage to answer.

"I thought you understood," he said.

"I understood that you were sick. I understood that you were scared. I understood that you didn't want people treating you differently."

Aoi's voice trembled.

"What I didn't understand was why you decided that disappearing was better than saying goodbye."

GC had no answer.

She placed the envelope on the rack beside him.

"I won't tell her your past. That's your decision."

He looked up.

"But if you keep making decisions for everyone else, don't be surprised when they start making their own."

Aoi walked away.

GC remained beside the bicycles.

The envelope sat between his hands.

He had spent years believing that leaving had been the kindest thing he could do.

He had never asked Aoi whether she agreed.

He had never asked anyone.

And now Hanako was asking questions he had no idea how to answer.

---

# CHAPTER 4
## Someone Who Knew Her Before

Mizuki Kanda joined the student council at the beginning of the month.

He was polite, organized, and infuriatingly good at noticing when Hanako had too much work.

Within three days, he had reorganized the council's filing system.

Within five, he had convinced two members to stop arguing over the budget.

Within seven, he had made Hanako laugh.

This was unforgivable.

"You're smiling," he said.

Hanako immediately straightened.

"I was thinking."

"About something funny?"

"About how much work you still have."

Mizuki smiled.

"There she is."

Hanako narrowed her eyes.

"What does that mean?"

"It means you look less terrifying when you're not trying to scare people."

"I don't try to scare people."

"Right."

"I don't."

"Of course."

She pointed at the paperwork.

"Finish those."

"Already done."

She looked down.

Every form was organized, labeled, and filed.

Hanako stared.

Mizuki rested his chin on his hand.

"Would you like me to reorganize your desk next?"

"Touch my desk and I'll reorganize your entire existence."

"That's the Hanako I remember."

She froze.

"Remember?"

He looked surprised by his own words.

"We were in the same middle school, weren't we?"

Hanako studied him.

Then she recognized him.

Mizuki Kanda. A quiet boy who had once helped her prepare for a debate competition. Someone who had listened without making her feel as if she had to defend every opinion.

They had lost contact after graduating.

"You've changed," she said.

"So have you."

"How?"

"You used to pretend that nothing bothered you."

Hanako looked away.

"I still do."

"Not as convincingly."

She should have been annoyed.

Instead, she found herself laughing.

It was an easy laugh, one that didn't require her to check whether GC was watching or wonder what her expression might reveal.

That realization made her stop.

Mizuki noticed.

"Something wrong?"

"No."

He didn't push.

And that, too, was familiar.

Later that afternoon, Mizuki helped her carry a stack of council documents to the staff room.

As they walked, he asked, "Are you seeing someone?"

Hanako almost dropped the papers.

"Why would you ask that?"

"You've been checking your phone every five minutes."

"I have responsibilities."

"Of course."

"And I can check my phone whenever I want."

"Absolutely."

She gave him a suspicious look.

"You're enjoying this."

"A little."

Hanako sighed.

"Yes. I'm seeing someone."

Mizuki's smile softened.

"That's good."

She waited for another question.

It never came.

For some reason, that bothered her more than if he had asked a dozen.

That evening, she met GC at the station.

He was late.

Only twelve minutes, but she noticed because he was almost never late when he had promised to meet her.

"Sorry," he said. "Something came up."

She studied him.

"With Aoi?"

He looked at her.

"Yes."

The answer was immediate.

Honest.

Still, something in Hanako tightened.

"Did you talk?"

"Yes."

"About your past?"

He hesitated.

"A little."

She nodded.

"Okay."

GC looked at her carefully.

"Are you angry?"

"No."

"Hanako."

She stopped walking.

"What do you want me to say?"

"I want to know what you're thinking."

She almost laughed.

It was such a simple request.

She wanted the same thing from him.

"I'm thinking that I don't know how to ask you questions without feeling like I'm demanding something you don't want to give me."

GC's expression softened.

"You can ask me anything."

"Will you answer?"

He opened his mouth.

Then closed it.

Hanako looked away.

"That's what I thought."

She walked ahead.

GC followed, but the distance between them remained.

# CHAPTER 5
## The First Lie That Wasn't a Lie

The school festival was three weeks away.

The student council was drowning in paperwork.

Hanako was drowning in everything else.

GC had become harder to reach.

Not absent.

Not deliberately cruel.

Just distant in a way that made her feel as though she were trying to hold a conversation with someone standing on the other side of a closed door.

He still walked her to the station.

He still remembered her favorite snacks.

He still asked whether she had eaten.

But he had stopped telling her things without being asked.

And when she asked, he gave answers that were technically true and somehow incomplete.

"How was your day?"

"Fine."

"Did you see Aoi?"

"Briefly."

"Did you talk about what happened before you transferred?"

"Some of it."

Every answer closed a door.

One evening, Hanako found him outside the school building, speaking to Aoi.

She wasn't trying to spy.

She had forgotten her student council notebook and returned to collect it.

They stood beneath the covered walkway, rain falling beyond the roof.

Aoi was holding the old envelope.

GC looked exhausted.

"You can't keep avoiding it," she said.

"I know."

"Then tell her."

"I will."

"When?"

GC rubbed his forehead.

"When I know how."

Aoi's expression hardened.

"That's what you said to me."

He went still.

She lowered her voice.

"Do you remember what happened after you left?"

GC looked toward the rain.

"I remember."

"No. You remember your side of it."

Hanako stopped behind the corner.

She knew she shouldn't listen.

She knew it was private.

But she had already heard too much to walk away without feeling as though she were abandoning something important.

Aoi continued.

"You think you were protecting everyone. You think if you disappeared, nobody would have to watch you struggle."

GC's voice was barely audible.

"I didn't want you to remember me like that."

"Like what?"

He didn't answer.

Aoi stepped closer.

"Like someone who was afraid?"

GC looked at her.

"Like someone who couldn't keep his promises."

For the first time, Aoi seemed to have no response.

Then she said, "You don't get to decide that for me."

She held out the envelope.

GC took it.

Their fingers touched.

Aoi looked at him with an expression Hanako had never seen on anyone's face before.

Not romantic longing.

Not exactly.

It was the pain of someone who had once been close enough to understand a person and had been left outside without knowing why.

Hanako backed away.

She didn't hear the rest.

She didn't see GC place the envelope in his bag.

She didn't know what was inside.

She only knew that Aoi had been part of a story GC had never told her.

And that he had promised to tell her.

Later, when GC met her at the station, he smiled as if nothing had happened.

"Sorry I'm late."

Hanako looked at him.

"Did you have a good conversation?"

His smile faded.

"What?"

"With Aoi."

He hesitated.

"Not really."

"Did she give you something?"

GC looked at her sharply.

Hanako's stomach dropped.

That was an answer.

Not to her question.

To the fact that she had seen something.

"Why are you asking?" he said.

She stared at him.

"Because I want to understand."

He looked exhausted.

"I know."

"Then let me."

He lowered his eyes.

"I can't yet."

Hanako felt the words land somewhere deep inside her.

Not because he had said no.

Because he had said *yet*.

As though her place in his life depended on the passage of time rather than her willingness to listen.

She turned away.

"Fine."

"Hanako—"

"Not now."

She walked toward the station.

GC didn't follow.

That night, she wrote a message.

*I don't need you to tell me everything. I just need to know whether you're keeping me around while you decide if you can trust me.*

She stared at it for several minutes.

Then deleted it.

She didn't want to be the kind of person who demanded answers.

She didn't want to become someone who confused love with ownership.

But she was beginning to understand how easy it was to use those principles as excuses to remain silent.

And she was afraid that both of them were doing exactly that.

# PART II
## SOMEONE WHO KNOWS HIM

# CHAPTER 6
## The Photograph

The photograph appeared on Friday.

Mizuki found Hanako staring at her phone in the student council room.

"Bad news?"

"No."

"That sounded convincing."

She locked the screen.

"Do you ever stop talking?"

"Occasionally. Usually when I'm asleep."

"Try it more often."

He pulled a chair over.

"You know you can tell me if something's wrong."

Hanako looked at him.

The words were ordinary.

She had heard them before.

But lately, ordinary kindness had begun to feel dangerous.

Because it made her aware of how lonely she had become while technically being in a relationship.

"I'm fine," she said.

Mizuki nodded.

He didn't believe her.

He also didn't argue.

Instead, he placed a bottle of water beside her paperwork.

"Drink something. You've been staring at that screen for ten minutes."

Hanako looked down.

She hadn't realized.

"Thank you."

"Don't mention it."

He returned to his desk.

Hanako unlocked her phone again.

The photograph was still there.

It had been sent by an unknown account.

No message.

No explanation.

Just an old picture of GC and Aoi sitting together in a hospital garden.

GC looked younger. Thinner. His hair was shorter, and his expression was tired.

Aoi sat beside him, holding his hand.

On the back of the photograph, visible through the scan, was a handwritten date from several years earlier.

Hanako zoomed in.

GC was smiling.

Not his usual small, restrained smile.

A genuine one.

A smile she had never seen before.

She hated herself for noticing.

She hated the unknown sender even more.

A second message arrived.

*Ask him why he left her.*

Hanako stared at the words.

Her first instinct was to show GC.

Her second was to delete the image and pretend it had never existed.

Her third, the one she was most ashamed of, was to ask herself whether he had ever looked at her the way he looked at Aoi in that photograph.

She closed the message.

Then she put her phone away.

That evening, she met GC after school.

He looked tired, but he smiled when he saw her.

"Hey."

"Hi."

They walked toward the station.

For several minutes, Hanako tried to decide how to begin.

Finally, she asked, "Were you close to Aoi?"

GC's pace slowed.

"Yes."

"How close?"

He looked at her.

"She was important to me."

The answer was honest.

It was also devastating.

Hanako swallowed.

"Did you love her?"

GC stopped walking.

For a moment, she thought he might deny it.

Instead, he said, "I don't know what word I would use for it back then."

Hanako looked at the ground.

"That's convenient."

His expression changed.

"Hanako."

"What?"

"Please don't ask me to explain something I haven't figured out myself."

She looked up sharply.

"I didn't ask you to explain everything. I asked whether you loved her."

"And I told you I don't know."

"Then tell me what you do know."

GC went quiet.

The silence stretched.

Hanako laughed once, without humor.

"Right."

She turned toward the station.

He caught up with her.

"She's part of my past."

"Then why does she still have so much power over your present?"

GC flinched.

Hanako regretted the words immediately.

But she couldn't take them back.

He looked at her, and for the first time in weeks, she saw something other than exhaustion in his expression.

Hurt.

"I didn't choose for her to come back," he said.

"I know."

"I didn't ask her to contact me."

"I know."

"Then why are you talking to me as if I did something wrong?"

Hanako had no answer.

Because he hadn't.

Not exactly.

And that was what frightened her.

She had no proof of betrayal.

Only a growing collection of unanswered questions.

"I'm sorry," she said quietly.

GC looked away.

"Me too."

They reached the station.

Neither reached for the other's hand.

# CHAPTER 7
## The Things Mizuki Didn't Ask

Hanako began spending more time with Mizuki.

At first, it was practical.

The student council was preparing for the festival, and he was efficient. He remembered deadlines, noticed mistakes, and didn't make her feel guilty for being tired.

Then it became something else.

She started staying in the council room after everyone left.

She started telling him stories about school that she hadn't bothered sharing with GC.

She told him about the first time she had been embarrassed in front of the entire class.

She told him about her parents' expectations.

She even told him about how difficult it was to admit that she liked someone.

She did not tell him GC's name.

Mizuki didn't ask.

One afternoon, he found her sitting alone on the stairs.

"Are you waiting for someone?"

"No."

"Then why are you sitting here?"

"Because I wanted to."

He sat one step below her.

For a while, they watched students cross the courtyard.

Then Mizuki said, "You don't have to pretend you're okay just because you don't want to make someone worry."

Hanako looked at him.

The words struck too close.

"Where did that come from?"

"I've known you for years."

"That doesn't mean you know everything."

"No," he agreed. "It doesn't."

She looked away.

He continued.

"But I know you used to get angry whenever people tried to help you. You thought accepting help meant admitting you couldn't handle things yourself."

Hanako didn't answer.

"You're better at it now," he said. "Mostly."

She gave him a weak glare.

"That was almost a compliment."

"It was a compliment. I just didn't want you getting arrogant."

She laughed.

Then her expression fell.

"I don't know what I'm doing anymore."

Mizuki turned toward her.

"With what?"

"Someone I care about."

He was quiet.

Hanako stared at her hands.

"I don't want to become someone who needs constant reassurance. I don't want to demand that he explain every private thing. But I also don't want to keep feeling like there's a part of his life I can never reach."

Mizuki listened.

"What would you tell him if he were sitting here?" he asked.

Hanako opened her mouth.

Then closed it.

She knew the answer.

*I want you to trust me.*

But that wasn't all.

*I want you to choose to tell me.*

And beneath those words was the one she was afraid to say.

*I'm scared that if you don't, someone else will always know you better than I do.*

She looked away.

"I don't know."

Mizuki nodded.

"Then maybe that's what you need to figure out before you talk to him."

Hanako sat beside him for a while longer.

When she finally stood, Mizuki stood, too.

"Thanks," she said.

"For what?"

"For not asking questions."

He smiled faintly.

"Sometimes questions aren't what people need."

Hanako walked back toward the student council room.

She felt lighter.

She also felt guilty.

Not because she had done anything wrong.

Because she had found it easier to speak honestly to someone who wasn't the person she loved.

And she knew that if she kept doing that, the distance between her and GC would not remain empty for long.

# CHAPTER 8
## The Envelope

GC opened the envelope on Sunday night.

He had spent three days carrying it around.

Three days placing it on his desk, putting it in his bag, taking it out again, and pretending he didn't know what was inside.

The paper was old.

His old name was written on the front in handwriting he recognized immediately.

Aoi's.

Inside was a letter.

He read it once.

Then again.

By the third time, he had stopped noticing the words individually.

They had become one long, unbroken accusation.

Not because Aoi had written anything cruel.

Because she had written the truth he had spent years avoiding.

*You don't have to pretend you're fine with me.*

*You don't have to make jokes when you're frightened.*

*You don't have to promise me you'll come back if you don't know whether you can.*

*But please stop making decisions for me.*

*I know you're scared.*

*I'm scared, too.*

*I don't want you to disappear.*

At the bottom, there was one final line.

*If you ever decide to tell me goodbye, let it be because you want to—not because you think I can't survive hearing it.*

GC lowered the letter.

He remembered the hospital.

He remembered the long months when every appointment seemed to carry another uncertainty.

He remembered Aoi sitting beside him in the garden, talking about ordinary things because she knew he hated being asked how he felt.

He remembered promising to come back to school.

He remembered failing to answer her messages.

He remembered the day he transferred.

He had told himself that he was protecting her from watching him struggle.

He had never asked what it had cost her.

His phone buzzed.

A message from Hanako.

**Hanako:** Can we talk tomorrow?

GC stared at it.

He wanted to tell her everything.

He wanted to explain the letter, the hospital, the fear, the guilt.

He wanted to tell her that Aoi had never been a secret romance he was trying to rekindle.

But the truth was more complicated than that.

Aoi had been his closest friend during the worst period of his life.

She had seen him when he was frightened, angry, and ashamed.

She had watched him struggle with the possibility that his future might look nothing like the one he had imagined.

And when he left, he had abandoned her without giving her the chance to decide whether she wanted to stay.

He had hurt someone who mattered to him.

He couldn't undo that.

He couldn't even explain it without admitting that the reason he had hidden his past from Hanako was the same reason he had disappeared from Aoi's life.

He believed that if he carried the pain alone, nobody else would have to.

His phone buzzed again.

**Hanako:** Please don't say you're busy.

GC closed his eyes.

Then he typed.

**GC:** Tomorrow. I'll tell you what I can.

He almost added *everything*.

He deleted the word before sending the message.

That small decision would haunt him.

# CHAPTER 9
## A Promise with a Missing Piece

They met in the library after school.

Hanako arrived early.

GC arrived on time.

Neither smiled.

She sat across from him, hands folded on the table.

"You said you'd tell me what happened."

"I will."

"Okay."

GC looked down.

"Aoi was my closest friend before I transferred. She was there during a difficult period of my medical treatment. She helped me through things I didn't know how to handle."

Hanako listened without interrupting.

"When I left, I stopped answering her messages. I told myself I was making it easier for her."

"Did she ask you to leave?"

"No."

"Did she tell you she wanted you gone?"

"No."

"Then why did you?"

GC's fingers tightened.

"Because I was scared."

The answer was so simple that Hanako didn't know what to do with it.

He continued.

"I was scared she would keep worrying about me. I was scared she would remember me as someone who couldn't do the things we planned to do. I thought if I left before things got worse, she wouldn't have to watch."

Hanako looked at him.

"And did that work?"

He shook his head.

"No."

"Then why are you doing the same thing to me?"

GC went still.

The question had found the part of him he had been trying not to examine.

"I don't want to," he said.

"But you are."

"I know."

She waited.

GC took a breath.

"I'm sorry."

Hanako's eyes lowered.

"Do you still have feelings for her?"

He hesitated.

"Not in the way you're asking."

"That isn't a yes or a no."

"I know."

Her jaw tightened.

"Do you want her back in your life?"

"She's already in it."

Hanako looked away.

The sentence was true.

It was also the sentence she had feared.

GC leaned forward.

"But I don't want to be with her. I want to be with you."

She looked at him.

"Then why do I feel like I'm the only person who doesn't know what's going on?"

He had no answer.

Because he had not told her about the letter.

He had not told her that Aoi had asked him to stop deciding what other people could handle.

He had not told her that reading those words had made him realize he was repeating the same mistake.

He had understood the lesson.

He had not yet found the courage to follow it.

Hanako stood.

"I believe you when you say you want to be with me."

GC looked up.

"But I don't know whether you know how to be with me."

She picked up her bag.

"That's what scares me."

He stood, too.

"Hanako, please."

She paused.

He wanted to say it.

He wanted to tell her about the letter.

Instead, he said, "I need a little more time."

Her expression emptied.

"Of course you do."

She walked away.

GC remained beside the table.

For the first time, he understood that a promise could be sincere and still feel like a rejection.

# PART III
## THINGS LEFT UNSAID

# CHAPTER 10
## The Festival

The school festival arrived beneath a cloudless sky.

Hanako had spent weeks organizing it.

She had arranged the booths, negotiated with teachers, resolved arguments, and threatened at least three people with administrative consequences.

Normally, the day would have filled her with pride.

Instead, she kept looking for GC.

He had promised to visit the student council booth.

He hadn't arrived.

At eleven, she checked her phone.

Nothing.

At eleven-thirty, she sent a message.

**Hanako:** Are you coming?

No response.

At noon, Mizuki found her standing behind the booth, staring at a stack of forms.

"You're going to burn a hole through those papers."

"I'm working."

"You're holding them upside down."

She looked down.

She was.

Mizuki gently took the papers from her.

"Go get some air."

"I can't. The booth—"

"I'll handle it."

She hesitated.

Then nodded.

She walked toward the courtyard.

Near the far gate, she saw GC.

He was standing with Aoi.

She was holding his bag while he adjusted something inside it.

They looked familiar together.

Not flirtatious.

Not even especially close.

Just familiar in the way people looked when they had shared difficult experiences long before anyone else had known their names.

Aoi said something.

GC smiled faintly.

Hanako stopped.

Then Aoi reached up and straightened the collar of his shirt.

It was a small gesture.

A gesture that meant nothing by itself.

But Hanako had spent weeks collecting small moments that meant nothing by themselves.

The late arrivals.

The unfinished explanations.

The messages that ended before she had learned what she needed to know.

The photograph.

The envelope.

The way GC's face changed whenever Aoi's name appeared.

Together, the moments became something she could no longer ignore.

She turned away.

"Hanako?"

Mizuki was standing behind her.

She hadn't heard him approach.

He looked toward the gate.

Then back at her.

"Do you want to go somewhere?"

She shook her head.

"No."

Her voice sounded steady.

That surprised her.

"I have work."

She walked back to the booth.

For the next two hours, she smiled at visitors, explained the festival schedule, and answered questions.

She did everything correctly.

Only once did she make a mistake.

When a student asked where to find the coffee stand, Hanako pointed toward the wrong building.

She corrected herself immediately.

Nobody noticed.

Except Mizuki.

At the end of the day, he found her sitting alone behind the booth.

"Did you talk to him?"

Hanako shook her head.

"Not yet."

"Are you going to?"

She looked at the empty courtyard.

"I don't know what I would say."

Mizuki sat beside her.

"Then maybe start with that."

She looked at him.

He didn't tell her that GC was wrong.

He didn't tell her to break up with him.

He didn't even tell her that she deserved better.

He simply stayed.

And Hanako found herself wondering why the person who had promised to stay was becoming the person she felt least able to reach.

# CHAPTER 11
## The Message That Arrived Too Late

GC had been at the festival.

He had arrived late because his appointment had run longer than expected. He had texted Hanako, but the message had failed to send.

By the time he realized, she had already left the courtyard.

He had found Aoi near the gate.

She had brought him a document his mother had asked her to return.

He had been exhausted.

She had noticed.

She had adjusted his collar because it had folded beneath his bag strap.

Nothing more had happened.

That was the truth.

But the truth, GC was learning, did not always arrive in time to protect anyone.

He searched for Hanako.

He checked the student council booth.

He asked Hiro.

He called her once.

No answer.

He sent a message.

**GC:** I'm sorry I missed you. My appointment ran late. Can we talk?

The message delivered.

She read it.

He knew because the status changed.

She did not reply.

That evening, Hiro found him sitting on the school steps.

"You look terrible."

"Thanks."

"You're welcome."

Hiro sat beside him.

"What happened?"

GC explained.

Hiro listened without interrupting.

When GC finished, Hiro sighed.

"Did you tell her about the appointment?"

"I tried."

"Did you tell her about Aoi?"

"Some of it."

"Some?"

GC looked away.

Hiro rubbed his forehead.

"You're making this really difficult."

"I know."

"No, I don't think you do. You keep acting as if the truth is a box you can open one piece at a time until everyone gets exactly the amount they can handle."

GC said nothing.

"That's not how people work," Hiro continued. "Especially not when they already feel shut out."

"I don't want to hurt her."

"Then stop making her guess."

GC's jaw tightened.

"You think I don't know that?"

"I think knowing something and doing something about it are two different skills."

GC stood abruptly.

"You're making it sound easy."

Hiro looked up at him.

"No. I'm making it sound necessary."

The words struck harder than GC expected.

He turned away.

Hiro sighed.

"I'm not saying you owe Hanako every detail of your life. You don't. But if something affects your relationship, you can't keep hiding behind the idea that you're protecting her."

GC's hands curled into fists.

"What if the truth makes everything worse?"

Hiro looked at him for a long time.

"Then at least she'll be allowed to decide what she wants to do with it."

GC didn't answer.

He left the school alone.

That night, he opened Aoi's letter again.

He read the final line.

*Let it be because you want to—not because you think I can't survive hearing it.*

For the first time, he wondered whether he had ever truly understood it.

# CHAPTER 12
## A Different Kind of Jealousy

Hanako met Mizuki at the library the following week.

They were supposed to be preparing the student council's festival report.

Instead, she spent twenty minutes staring at the same paragraph.

Mizuki closed his notebook.

"Do you want to talk about it?"

"No."

"Okay."

He opened the notebook again.

Hanako stared at him.

"That's it?"

"You said no."

"You're supposed to ask again."

"Why?"

She opened her mouth.

Nothing came out.

Mizuki watched her quietly.

Then he said, "Hanako, I can listen if you want me to. But I don't want you to feel like you have to explain yourself just because I'm here."

She looked down.

That was precisely why she wanted to explain.

He made it easy.

Too easy.

"I'm having problems with someone I care about," she said.

Mizuki nodded.

"I guessed."

"How?"

"You've been pretending not to check your phone for a week."

She sighed.

"Is it that obvious?"

"Only to people who know you."

She looked at him.

"Do you ever wish you had done something differently? Something that seemed reasonable at the time, but later you realized it hurt someone?"

"Yes."

"What did you do?"

"I apologized."

"What if apologizing doesn't fix it?"

Mizuki thought for a moment.

"Then you have to decide whether you're apologizing because you want the other person to forgive you or because you recognize what you did."

Hanako looked away.

He continued.

"Those aren't always the same thing."

She felt the words settle inside her.

She had been so busy wondering whether GC was hiding something that she hadn't considered how her own silence might be changing him.

She had stopped telling him about her day.

She had started answering his messages with one-word replies.

She had begun spending time with Mizuki because it was easier than admitting how lonely she felt.

She hadn't betrayed GC.

But she had been moving away from him.

One small decision at a time.

Mizuki closed his notebook.

"Can I ask you something?"

She nodded.

"Do you want me to be the person you talk to because you trust me, or because talking to him has become too difficult?"

Hanako stared at him.

The question hurt.

Not because it was cruel.

Because it was fair.

"I don't know," she whispered.

Mizuki nodded.

"Then I think you should figure that out before we become something you use to avoid dealing with him."

She looked at him sharply.

"Are you saying you don't want to be my friend?"

"No."

His voice was gentle.

"I'm saying I like you enough not to pretend I don't have feelings about this."

Hanako went still.

He looked away.

"I've liked you for a long time. I didn't expect anything to happen. And I'm not going to ask you to choose me because you're unhappy with someone else."

She couldn't speak.

Mizuki stood.

"You don't owe me an answer. I just didn't want to lie about why I've been here."

He picked up his bag.

"Take your time."

Then he left.

Hanako sat alone in the library.

For the first time, she understood that kindness could become a temptation.

Not because Mizuki had done anything wrong.

Because he had offered her a kind of closeness that asked nothing from her except honesty.

And she had begun to crave it.

# CHAPTER 13
## What Aoi Came Back For

GC found Aoi at the station.

She was sitting on the bench where she had first spoken to Hanako.

He stopped in front of her.

"Why did you come back?"

Aoi looked up.

"I told you."

"You said you wanted to see me."

"I did."

"That's not all."

She studied his face.

Then she sighed.

"No. It isn't."

She moved her bag aside.

GC sat down.

For a while, neither spoke.

Then Aoi reached inside her bag and took out another envelope.

This one was newer.

"My mother found it while sorting some old medical papers. It was addressed to you, but it never got sent."

GC looked at the envelope.

"From whom?"

"Your mother."

He stared at her.

"Why do you have it?"

"Because she asked me to bring it to you. She didn't want to send it through the post."

GC took the envelope.

His mother's handwriting was unmistakable.

He opened it.

The letter was short.

It explained that during the period before his transfer, she had been worried about how much he was trying to hide. She had thought he needed a fresh start, but she had not understood how much leaving would hurt him.

The final paragraph made his hands go cold.

*I know you believed that leaving your friends would make things easier for them. I let you believe that because I didn't know how to tell you that I was frightened, too.*

*I should have told you that you were allowed to need people.*

*I am sorry I didn't.*

GC lowered the letter.

Aoi looked at him.

"I didn't come back to take you away from anyone."

He didn't speak.

"I came because I was tired of being the person you left behind without an explanation."

GC's throat tightened.

"I'm sorry."

"I know."

"I should have called."

"Yes."

"I should have told you."

"Yes."

He looked at her.

"Are you going to forgive me?"

Aoi's expression softened, but she shook her head.

"That's not why I came."

He looked confused.

"I don't want to spend the rest of my life waiting for you to decide whether I'm allowed to be hurt," she said. "I wanted you to know what happened. After that, I wanted to go home."

GC stared at her.

"You came all this way just to say goodbye?"

Aoi smiled sadly.

"No. I came to see whether you were still the boy who thought saying goodbye was too painful to do properly."

She stood.

"I think I have my answer."

GC remained seated.

Aoi adjusted the strap of her bag.

"Tell Hanako the truth."

He looked up.

"She doesn't deserve to become another person you leave behind."

Aoi walked away.

GC watched her until she disappeared into the crowd.

For years, he had believed that his past was a burden he had to carry alone.

Now he understood something worse.

By hiding it, he had made other people carry the consequences without ever knowing why.

He took out his phone.

He typed a message to Hanako.

*I need to tell you everything.*

He stared at it.

Then he added:

*Can we meet tonight?*

He pressed send before he could change his mind.

For the first time in weeks, he felt afraid of the truth.

Not because he thought it would hurt Hanako.

Because he knew he had already hurt her by waiting.

# PART IV
## THE TRUTH IS NOT ENOUGH

# CHAPTER 14
## The Last Coffee Before the Storm

Hanako agreed to meet.

They chose a small café near the station.

She arrived first.

GC arrived five minutes later, carrying no bag and looking as though he had spent the entire day rehearsing a conversation he still didn't know how to begin.

He sat across from her.

For a moment, neither spoke.

Then GC said, "I owe you an explanation."

Hanako looked at him.

"Yes."

He nodded.

"Aoi and I were close. Very close. She was the person I talked to when I was frightened about my health. She knew things I didn't tell anyone else."

Hanako's fingers tightened around her cup.

"Did you love her?"

GC looked down.

"I loved her."

The words landed between them.

Hanako's expression did not change.

GC continued quickly.

"But I don't think it was the kind of love you're asking about. I don't know whether I understood the difference back then. She was important to me, and I was important to her. We were both scared, and we relied on each other."

Hanako stared at the table.

"Why didn't you tell me that?"

"Because I was ashamed."

"Of loving her?"

"Of leaving her."

He told her about the hospital, the transfer, the unanswered messages, and the letter he had kept hidden.

He told her how he had convinced himself that disappearing would make things easier.

He told her that Aoi had returned because she wanted him to acknowledge what he had done rather than keep pretending it had been an act of kindness.

He told her everything.

Or nearly everything.

He did not tell her about the photograph.

He did not know who had sent it.

He did not tell her that he had seen the message that followed.

He had deleted it before showing anyone.

He wanted to believe that the photograph was an attempt to create trouble rather than evidence of something he still did not understand.

He wanted to believe that one complete explanation could repair the weeks of silence.

When he finished, Hanako sat very still.

Finally, she asked, "Why did you think I couldn't handle this?"

"I didn't think you couldn't handle it."

"Then why did you hide it?"

GC looked at her.

He had no answer that could make the question less painful.

Hanako nodded slowly.

"Okay."

He frowned.

"Okay?"

"I understand what happened."

Relief flickered across his face.

Then she continued.

"But I don't understand why you made me feel like I had to compete with someone from your past just to get you to talk to me."

His relief disappeared.

"I wasn't asking you to compete."

"I know."

"Hanako—"

"I know you weren't doing it deliberately."

She looked at him.

"That's what makes it so difficult."

GC fell silent.

Hanako took a breath.

"I believe that you loved her. I believe you were scared. I believe you thought you were protecting her."

She paused.

"But you keep treating honesty like something you can give me after you've decided the danger has passed."

GC stared at her.

"I didn't know how to explain it."

"You could have started by saying you didn't know how."

He looked down.

She continued, her voice quiet.

"I would have listened. I would have been confused, and probably jealous, and I might have said something stupid. But I would have listened."

GC's eyes lowered.

"I know."

"No, you don't."

The words came out sharper than she intended.

She closed her eyes.

"I'm sorry."

He shook his head.

"Don't be."

She looked at him.

For a moment, they were the people they had been in Volume 1: two awkward students trying to find the right words.

Only now, the silence between them had a history.

Hanako reached for her bag.

"I need time."

GC nodded.

"Okay."

She stood.

He remained seated.

She wanted him to follow.

She wanted him to say something that would make the room feel less cold.

She wanted to be able to trust him without having to fight herself for it.

But he didn't follow.

And she couldn't decide whether that was respect or another way of letting her leave.

# CHAPTER 15
## The Photograph's Owner

The unknown account messaged Hanako again.

This time, it sent a name.

**Aoi Mizuhara.**

Then a second message.

*You heard his explanation. Did he tell you what happened the night he left?*

Hanako stared at the screen.

She had promised herself she wouldn't investigate GC's past behind his back.

She had promised herself she would ask him directly.

She had promised herself a great many things.

But the message had found the one place his explanation had left open.

The night he left.

GC had told her that he transferred schools after a difficult period of medical treatment.

He had told her he stopped answering Aoi's messages.

He had not told her what happened on the final night.

She typed:

*Who is this?*

The reply came quickly.

*Someone who knows why Aoi waited for him.*

Hanako's pulse quickened.

*Then tell me.*

The account went silent.

She waited ten minutes.

Then twenty.

No answer.

She put the phone away.

That evening, she went to GC's house.

She had never visited before.

She stood outside the gate for several minutes before ringing the bell.

GC's mother answered.

"Oh, Hanako."

She looked surprised, then concerned.

"Is something wrong?"

Hanako hesitated.

"I need to talk to GC."

"He isn't home yet. He has an appointment."

The words sent a familiar chill through her.

"Is he okay?"

"Yes. It's a routine follow-up."

Hanako nodded.

Then GC's mother looked at her more carefully.

"Would you like to wait?"

Hanako wanted to say yes.

Instead, she shook her head.

"No. I think I should speak to him first."

She bowed and walked away.

Halfway down the street, her phone buzzed.

An unknown number.

A message.

*You should ask him what he promised Aoi before he left.*

Another message arrived.

*And why he thinks he broke it.*

The mystery had not ended when GC told her the truth.

It had only changed shape.

She wanted to call him.

She wanted to ask him what the messages meant.

But a new fear had begun to grow beneath her anger.

What if GC had told her the truth as he understood it?

What if he genuinely believed that he had explained everything?

What if the part he had left out was the part he didn't know?

She typed a message.

*There's something else I need to ask you.*

She didn't send it.

Instead, she put her phone away and kept walking.

For the first time, she understood why GC had been so afraid of the past.

Not because it was necessarily terrible.

Because once you opened the door, you couldn't choose what came through it.

# CHAPTER 16
## The Night Nobody Remembered Correctly

GC found the photograph in an old folder on his mother's computer.

He had searched for it after receiving a message from an unknown number.

The image was the same one Hanako had seen.

GC and Aoi in the hospital garden.

Her hand around his.

His head turned toward her.

The date was familiar.

The day before he transferred.

He remembered that day.

At least, he thought he did.

He remembered sitting in the garden after a difficult appointment. He remembered Aoi talking about school. He remembered telling her he would come back.

He remembered promising.

What he didn't remember was the argument that followed.

His mother had told him about it once, years later, but he had been too angry to listen.

Now he searched through the old messages.

Most were ordinary.

Then he found one from Aoi.

*You said you would tell me before you left.*

Another.

*Please answer.*

Another.

*I don't understand why you won't talk to me.*

The final message was dated the night before he transferred.

*If you really want me out of your life, at least tell me yourself.*

GC stared at the screen.

He remembered reading those messages.

He remembered deciding not to answer.

But he had never considered that Aoi might have believed he had chosen to end their friendship.

He had told himself that he was protecting her.

She had been left to interpret his silence as rejection.

The phone rang.

His mother.

"Did you find what you were looking for?"

"Did I promise Aoi I would tell her before I left?"

A pause.

"Yes."

GC closed his eyes.

"Why didn't you tell me?"

"I did."

"When?"

"After you transferred. You were angry. You said you didn't want to talk about it."

He gripped the edge of the desk.

"What happened that night?"

His mother sighed.

"You had been told that your treatment plan might change. You were frightened. You argued with Aoi because she wanted you to tell her what was happening, and you didn't want her to see how scared you were."

GC stared at the old messages.

"I told her I would come back."

"Yes."

"And then I left."

"Yes."

He lowered his head.

For years, he had believed he had left to protect Aoi from the burden of watching him struggle.

Now he understood that the story had another side.

He had promised her honesty.

When he became frightened, he had broken that promise.

Then he had rewritten the memory until leaving looked like kindness instead of fear.

His mother spoke softly.

"GC, I know you were young. I know you were scared. I never thought you were trying to hurt her."

He swallowed.

"But you did hurt her," his mother continued. "And being scared doesn't change that."

He stared at the screen.

The truth was not that he had been secretly cruel.

It was worse.

He had been a frightened boy who had made a decision for someone else and called it love.

And he had spent years repeating the same mistake.

# CHAPTER 17
## The Person Who Waited

Aoi agreed to meet GC one last time.

They chose the hospital garden.

It had changed since they were younger. The benches had been replaced, and a new building blocked part of the view.

But the trees remained.

Aoi arrived carrying no envelope.

GC stood when he saw her.

"I remember now."

She nodded.

"Your mother told you?"

"And I found the messages."

Aoi looked away.

"I wondered if you ever would."

"I'm sorry."

"I know."

"I promised I'd tell you before I left."

"Yes."

"I didn't."

"No."

He swallowed.

"I thought I was protecting you."

Aoi looked at him.

"You were protecting yourself."

The words were quiet.

GC didn't argue.

She continued.

"You were afraid I would see you as weak. You were afraid I would worry. You were afraid that if you let me stay, I would see things you didn't want anyone to see."

He looked down.

"Yes."

"So you left first."

"Yes."

Aoi sat on the bench.

"Yes."

"Longer than I should have."

GC lowered his eyes.

"I'm sorry."

"I don't want you to spend the rest of your life apologizing to me."

"I want you to stop using the same excuse with everyone you love."

"Hanako?"

"GC, I don't know your relationship with her. I don't know what she's thinking. But if you keep treating honesty like a confession you make only after you've been caught, you're going to lose people who would have stayed if you'd simply trusted them."

"I don't know how to fix it."

Aoi noticed.

"That doesn't mean you shouldn't try to be honest."

She stood.

"I didn't come back because I wanted you to choose me."

"I came back because I wanted to stop waiting for you to become someone who could say goodbye."

"I think I can do that now."

"Are we still friends?"

Aoi looked at him for a long moment.

"I don't know."

He nodded.

It hurt.

But he understood.

She had the right not to know.

Aoi turned toward the path.

Then paused.

"One more thing."

GC looked up.

"Don't tell Hanako that I said she should forgive you."

"I won't."

"Good. She doesn't owe you that."

Aoi walked away.

GC watched her go.

This time, he did not mistake her departure for a punishment.

She was simply choosing her own life.

He wished he had learned to let people do that years ago.

# PART V
## GOODBYE, FOR NOW

# CHAPTER 18
## The Wrong Question

Hanako and GC met after school.

They chose the courtyard where she had first told him she cared about him.

Neither mentioned it.

GC arrived early.

Hanako arrived on time.

He looked at her.

"Thank you for coming."

"Don't make it sound like a favor."

"Sorry."

She gave him a tired look.

He stopped apologizing.

For a while, they stood beneath the trees.

Then GC said, "I found out what happened the night I left."

Hanako looked at him.

He told her about the promise, the argument, and the messages he had ignored.

He told her how he had convinced himself that leaving was kindness.

He told her that he had been wrong.

He did not ask her to forgive him.

When he finished, Hanako looked away.

"Why didn't you tell me that before?"

"I didn't remember it clearly. I didn't want to admit that I had been wrong."

She nodded.

It was an honest answer.

A painful one.

"Did you ever think about going back?"

"Yes."

"Did you want to?"

"Yes."

"Then why didn't you?"

GC looked at her.

"Because I thought it would be easier to start over somewhere else."

Hanako let out a small breath.

"That's what you always do."

He lowered his eyes.

"I know."

"No, I don't think you do."

Her voice shook.

"You keep telling me that you want me in your life. You keep telling me that you want to be honest. But every time something gets difficult, you decide what I can handle and leave me to deal with whatever you don't tell me."

GC didn't interrupt.

"I don't need you to be perfect," she continued. "I don't need you to know what to say. I don't even need you to explain everything immediately."

She looked at him.

"I need to know that when you're frightened, you won't decide that I'm better off without the truth."

GC swallowed.

"I want to learn how to do that."

"I believe you."

He looked up.

For one fragile second, hope appeared on his face.

Then Hanako continued.

"But I don't know if I can keep being the person you practice on."

The hope disappeared.

GC stared at her.

She had never seen him look so lost.

It was the expression she had once wanted to understand.

Now she was frightened by how familiar it had become.

"Hanako," he said quietly, "what are you saying?"

She looked down at her hands.

"I think I need to stop."

"Stop what?"

She almost laughed.

Even now, he was asking her to say the difficult thing.

She raised her eyes.

"Us."

The word seemed to change the air between them.

GC didn't move.

Hanako forced herself to continue.

"I love you. That's the problem. I keep thinking that if I love you enough, if I wait long enough, if I ask the right questions, you'll finally believe that I can handle the truth."

Her voice broke.

"And I can't keep doing that."

GC looked at the ground.

For a moment, she thought he might argue.

He didn't.

"Is there someone else?" he asked.

Hanako closed her eyes.

It was the wrong question.

And she understood why he had asked it.

If there was someone else, then perhaps the ending would have a simple explanation.

Perhaps he could blame Mizuki.

Perhaps she could blame Aoi.

Perhaps they could turn their pain into a competition and pretend that one of them had lost to someone better.

"No," she said.

GC looked up.

"I've been spending time with Mizuki. He told me he likes me."

GC's face tightened.

Hanako continued quickly.

"I haven't promised him anything. I haven't kissed him. I haven't chosen him over you."

She swallowed.

"But I found myself wanting to talk to him because it was easier than talking to you. And I hated that. I hated that I was becoming someone who hid things, too."

GC looked away.

"Do you like him?"

"I don't know."

The answer was honest.

It was also the first time she had ever said something to GC that she knew would hurt him and could not soften.

"I like being around him. I like that I don't have to guess what he means. I like that when I ask him something, he doesn't look at me as if I'm asking for more than he can give."

GC's eyes lowered.

"But I love you," she whispered. "And I don't know what to do with that anymore."

He stood very still.

Then he said, "I don't want to lose you."

Hanako's face crumpled for half a second before she forced it back into place.

"I know."

"I can change."

"I know."

"I can tell you everything."

"I know."

"Then why—"

"Because I don't want to stay until I stop believing you."

The words silenced him.

Hanako wiped at her eyes.

"I don't want our relationship to become a place where every promise sounds like an apology. I don't want to spend the next year wondering what you're not telling me. And I don't want you to stay with me because you're afraid I'll leave."

GC looked at her.

"What do you want me to do?"

She shook her head.

"I don't know."

For once, she was not asking him to find the answer.

She was admitting she didn't have one.

GC looked at the space between them.

Then he nodded.

"Okay."

Hanako stared at him.

"Okay?"

He swallowed.

"I don't want this. But I don't want you to stay because you feel guilty, either."

She felt something inside her break.

He was finally understanding.

Just not in time.

GC took a step back.

"Can I ask you one thing?"

She nodded.

"Did you ever believe me?"

Hanako's breath caught.

She could have said yes.

She could have made the ending easier.

Instead, she told him the truth.

"Sometimes."

He closed his eyes.

She continued.

"And sometimes I believed you wanted me to believe you more than you wanted to tell me the truth."

He nodded once.

It was the most painful answer she could have given him.

It was also the one he had earned.

They stood there for a long time.

Neither reached for the other's hand.

Finally, Hanako whispered, "I'm sorry."

GC looked at her.

"Don't apologize for being hurt."

She almost stepped toward him.

Almost.

Then she turned and walked away.

This time, GC did not follow.

---

# CHAPTER 19
## The Empty Desk

The next morning, Hanako bought two cans of coffee.

She stopped herself before reaching GC's classroom.

For several seconds, she stood outside the door, holding the cans.

She could still give him one.

She could place it on his desk and walk away.

She could pretend that the habit meant nothing.

But it did mean something.

That was why she couldn't do it.

She put both cans in her bag and went to class.

GC arrived a few minutes later.

He looked at her.

She looked at him.

Neither spoke.

Hiro noticed immediately.

He didn't make a joke.

That frightened Hanako more than anything else could have.

At lunch, GC sat beside Hiro.

Hanako sat with the student council members.

Mizuki glanced at her.

"Are you okay?"

"No."

He nodded.

"Do you want to talk?"

She shook her head.

"Not yet."

He accepted the answer.

Hanako looked down at her lunch.

She had spent months imagining what it would feel like to be GC's girlfriend.

She had never imagined how much ordinary life would hurt after it ended.

The empty seat beside her.

The messages she no longer had a reason to send.

The jokes she wanted to tell him.

The way she still looked for him when something happened.

It wasn't dramatic.

Nobody stopped the world.

Nobody announced that they had broken up.

The school continued.

The bells rang.

The teachers assigned homework.

Hiro made one joke during class, then stopped when nobody laughed.

And GC sat at his desk with no coffee beside his notebook.

---

# CHAPTER 20
## Hiro's Worst Joke

Hiro found GC on the roof after school.

He was sitting against the wall, looking at his phone.

"You're avoiding me."

GC put the phone away.

"I'm sitting in a public place."

"You're avoiding me in a public place."

GC almost smiled.

It disappeared quickly.

Hiro sat beside him.

For several minutes, neither spoke.

Then Hiro said, "I heard."

GC nodded.

"Yeah."

"Are you going to tell me what happened?"

GC looked at him.

"Some of it."

Hiro sighed.

"Of course."

GC stared at his hands.

"I thought I was doing better."

"You were."

"I thought I was learning."

"You were."

"Then why did I do it again?"

Hiro looked out across the school grounds.

"Because learning something doesn't mean you stop being afraid of it."

GC didn't answer.

Hiro continued.

"But you can't keep using fear as an excuse to make decisions for people."

"I know."

"Do you?"

GC looked at him.

Hiro's expression was serious.

"I've defended you before. I've told people you were just private, that you needed time, that you weren't trying to shut them out."

GC looked down.

"I know."

"I meant it. I still think you deserve privacy. But sometimes I think I helped you hide."

GC shook his head.

"You were trying to help."

"Maybe."

Hiro leaned back against the wall.

"But I don't want to keep helping you do something that hurts you."

GC's eyes stung.

He looked away.

"I didn't want to lose her."

Hiro's voice softened.

"I know."

"I thought if I told her everything, she'd look at me differently."

"She probably would have."

GC looked up.

Hiro shrugged.

"People look at each other differently when they learn something important. That's not always a bad thing."

GC swallowed.

"I didn't know how to let her see that part of me."

"Then you should have told her that."

GC closed his eyes.

Hiro nudged his shoulder lightly.

"I'm not saying you could have saved the relationship. Maybe you couldn't. Maybe she would still have been hurt. Maybe things would have gone wrong for a hundred other reasons."

He paused.

"But you don't get to know what might have happened if you never give anyone the chance."

GC sat silently.

After a while, Hiro stood.

"Come on."

"Where?"

"Home."

"I'm not a child."

"Then stop sitting on rooftops like a tragic protagonist."

GC let out a weak laugh.

Hiro smiled.

There was no magic in it.

No promise that things would be fine.

Just the familiar presence of a friend who had decided to stay, even when he couldn't fix anything.

As they walked down the stairs, GC said, "Hiro?"

"Yeah?"

"Do you think she hates me?"

Hiro stopped.

Then he shook his head.

"No."

GC looked down.

"That might be worse."

Hiro didn't disagree.

# CHAPTER 21
## The Girl Who Didn't Hate Him

Hanako did not hate GC.

She tried.

It would have been easier.

If she hated him, she could turn every memory into evidence. She could decide that the coffee, the hand-holding, the awkward confession, and the broken pen had all been mistakes.

She could tell herself that she had loved an illusion.

But GC had been real.

The boy who made her laugh.

The boy who remembered her favorite drink.

The boy who had sat beside her in the library and apologized without demanding forgiveness.

The boy who had wanted to be honest and had still been afraid.

She could not turn him into a villain just because he had hurt her.

That was the cruelest part.

On Friday afternoon, she found the two cans of coffee still in her bag.

She took them out.

One was the brand GC usually drank.

The other was hers.

She sat at her desk and stared at them.

Then she opened her own.

The sound was small.

Ordinary.

She took a sip.

It tasted exactly as it always had.

For some reason, she began to cry.

Not loudly.

Not dramatically.

She simply sat there with the can in her hand, trying to understand how something so ordinary could become a reminder of a person who was no longer hers to worry about.

A knock sounded at the classroom door.

Mizuki stood there.

He looked at her face, then at the coffee.

He didn't ask whether it was GC's.

He didn't ask whether she was still in love with him.

He simply approached and sat in the chair beside her.

Hanako wiped her eyes.

"Don't say anything."

"I won't."

She looked at him.

"Not even something comforting."

"Especially not that."

She let out a small, broken laugh.

Then she covered her face.

Mizuki stayed.

After a while, Hanako said, "I don't know if I'll ever stop loving him."

Mizuki looked at the floor.

"I know."

"I don't want you to think I'm asking you to wait."

"I'm not."

She lowered her hands.

He met her gaze.

"I like you, Hanako. But I don't want to become the person you choose because you're trying to forget someone else."

She nodded.

"I know."

"I can be your friend. If that's what you want."

Hanako looked at him for a long time.

Then she said, "I think that's what I need right now."

Mizuki nodded.

"Okay."

He stood.

At the door, he paused.

"For what it's worth, I don't think loving someone makes you foolish."

Hanako looked down.

"But I think you should be allowed to be happy without having to prove that you were right to love them."

He left.

Hanako sat alone.

The coffee had gone cold.

She drank it anyway.

# EPILOGUE
## The Message Without a Reply

Three weeks passed.

Hanako stopped checking her phone every five minutes.

She stopped buying two cans of coffee.

She stopped looking toward the classroom door whenever someone walked past.

Mostly.

GC began attending his appointments without hiding them from Hiro. He told his mother when he was frightened. He started writing things down when he couldn't find the words to say them aloud.

He was trying.

That did not mean Hanako knew about it.

He didn't tell her.

He wasn't sure whether he still had the right.

Aoi returned home.

Before leaving, she sent him one final message.

**Aoi:** I hope you learn to stay when you're scared.

GC read it several times.

Then he replied.

**GC:** I'm trying.

Aoi sent no further message.

He accepted that.

Some relationships did not end with hatred.

Some ended because the people involved had finally stopped asking each other to wait.

On the last Friday of the month, GC opened the small box he kept beneath his desk.

Inside were the empty coffee cans, folded notes, and broken pen.

He held the pen for a long time.

Then he placed it back.

He didn't throw anything away.

He didn't know whether that was healthy or foolish.

He only knew that he wanted to remember the truth.

Hanako had loved him.

He had loved her.

And he had hurt her while trying to protect himself from the possibility of being seen.

He could not change that.

But he could stop repeating it.

That evening, he wrote a message.

**GC:** I hope you're doing okay.

He stared at the words.

He wanted to add more.

*I miss you.*

*I'm sorry.*

*I wish I had told you everything sooner.*

*I don't know whether I deserve another chance.*

He typed none of them.

He sent the message.

Hanako read it on the train home.

She stared at the screen for a long time.

Then she locked her phone.

She didn't reply.

Not because she hated him.

Not because she wanted to punish him.

Because she didn't know what she could say that wouldn't reopen something she was still trying to understand.

The train carried her past the station where they had first held hands.

She looked out the window.

For the first time in weeks, she let herself remember the good parts without immediately searching for the moment they had gone wrong.

She remembered GC laughing at Hiro's ridiculous jokes.

She remembered the first coffee can.

She remembered his hand closing around hers.

She remembered the day he had asked whether he could kiss her.

She remembered telling him to stay.

Then she remembered the courtyard.

The empty space between them.

The word *us* falling apart.

Her phone remained silent.

She put it away.

Somewhere across the city, GC was probably doing the same thing.

They were both trying to learn how to live with a truth they had once believed they could avoid.

They had loved each other.

They were no longer together.

Neither fact erased the other.

# FINAL CHAPTER
## The Name Beneath the Name

The message arrived at 11:47 p.m.

GC was already in bed when his phone vibrated.

Unknown number.

He almost ignored it.

Then he saw the attached photograph.

It showed a hospital corridor.

A younger GC stood beside a woman he didn't recognize. Aoi was visible in the background, speaking to someone outside the frame.

The date was from the year he transferred.

His stomach tightened.

A second image arrived.

A handwritten document.

His old name appeared at the top.

Beneath it was a date, a signature, and a sentence he could barely bring himself to read.

*In the event that the patient is unable to communicate his wishes, contact the person listed below.*

The name beneath his own was unfamiliar.

The address was not.

It belonged to a hospital he had never mentioned to Hanako.

GC sat up.

His phone vibrated again.

**Unknown:** You finally remembered Aoi.

He stared at the message.

Another arrived.

**Unknown:** Now remember why you left.

GC's fingers went cold.

He typed:

*Who are you?*

The response came almost immediately.

**Unknown:** Someone who was there that night.

A third photograph appeared.

It showed the same corridor, the same date, and a door with a room number.

GC recognized the number.

He remembered the room.

He remembered the fear.

But the memory that followed was incomplete, like a page torn from a book.

He had always believed that he left because he was frightened of being seen as weak.

He had believed that Aoi was the person he had hurt by disappearing.

He had believed that his mother was the only other person who knew the full story.

Now someone was telling him there had been another witness.

Someone who had kept the truth for years.

Someone who had waited until after Hanako was gone.

His phone vibrated one final time.

**Unknown:** Don't contact Aoi.

Then:

**Unknown:** And don't tell Hanako.

GC stared at the screen.

For a moment, the old instinct returned.

Hide it.

Wait.

Figure it out alone.

Protect everyone else from the truth until he knew what it meant.

His thumb hovered over the message.

Then he stopped.

He opened Hanako's conversation.

Her last message to him was still there, weeks old.

He could tell her.

He could ask her to listen.

He could give her the chance to decide whether she wanted to be involved.

But he had no right to assume she would answer.

He had no right to make her responsible for a mystery he had spent years avoiding.

And he had no idea whether telling her would protect her or pull her into something she had never asked to be part of.

GC closed the conversation.

Not because he had decided to hide the truth.

Because, for the first time, he understood that choosing to tell someone was not the same as choosing for them.

He needed to find out who had sent the photographs.

He needed to understand what had happened that night.

And he needed to learn whether the memories he had spent years trusting were actually his own.

Across the city, Hanako woke briefly when her phone lit up.

No new message.

She turned it face down and went back to sleep.

She did not know that GC was staring at an unfamiliar name.

She did not know that the girl who had returned from his past might not be the person who understood it best.

And she did not know that the breakup she had believed was the end of their story had occurred just before the beginning of a much older one.

GC looked at the last photograph again.

At the bottom of the document was a second signature.

A name he recognized.

His mother's.

And beneath it, written in handwriting that was unmistakably his own, were four words:

*I don't remember agreeing.*

He read them once.

Then again.

Outside, rain began tapping against the window.

For the first time in his life, GC was afraid of a memory he hadn't yet recovered.

And somewhere in the city, someone who knew the truth was waiting for him to remember.

**The girl who had hated everyone had finally stopped chasing him.**

**Now, for the first time, he had no idea whether the truth would bring her back—or prove that leaving her had only been the beginning of his mistakes.**
