AfterwordA Small Thing, Repeated

Kuch stories grand gestures ke liye yaad rakhi jaati hain.

Baarish mein dramatic confession. Fireworks ke neeche kiya gaya promise. Koi insaan poora shehar paar kar jaata hai kyunki woh kisi baat ko kahe bina chhod nahi sakta.

Is story mein bhi woh feelings hain, lekin iske sabse important gestures chhote hain.

Kisi ke girne se pehle haath badha dena.

Aisi apology jo forgiveness ki demand nahi karti.

Jawab assume kiye bina sawaal poochhna.

Woh friend jo jaanta hai ki joke kab band karna hai.

Woh ladka jo kisi aur ke notice karne se pehle kehna seekhta hai, “I'm not feeling well.”

Aur desk par rakha coffee ka ek can.

Hanako kabhi quiet insaan nahi bani. Kaito bhi aisa insaan nahi bana jo har feeling ko perfectly explain kar sake. Dating shuru karne se unki problems gayab nahi hui, aur affection ne unhe zindagi ke difficult parts se immune nahi banaya.

Jo badla, woh tha pretend karna band karne ki unki willingness.

Usne seekha ki control kiye bina bhi care kiya ja sakta hai.

Usne seekha ki care accept karne ke badle kisi ko apni poori zindagi ka qarzdaar banana zaroori nahi.

Aur coffee cans, arguments, aur toote pens ke beech unhe samajh aaya ki samjhe jaane ke liye perfectly understood hona zaroori nahi.

Kabhi-kabhi bas doosre insaan ko koshish karne ka mauka dena kaafi hota hai.

Wahi unki story ki shuruaat thi.

Woh pal nahi jab unhone kiss kiya.

Woh pal bhi nahi jab unhone apni feelings confess ki.

Woh pal jab unhone ek-doosre ke liye yeh decide karna band kiya ki kaun kya handle kar sakta hai—and ek-doosre ko apna faisla khud karne diya.

One can of coffee. One honest answer. One day at a time.

Shuru karne ke liye itna kaafi tha.

— Kaito
