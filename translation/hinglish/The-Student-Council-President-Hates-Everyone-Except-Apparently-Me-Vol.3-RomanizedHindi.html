PrologueThe Message He Finally Sent

Kaito Mizuno ne saalon tak yeh maana tha ki khamosh rehna bhi ek tarah ki kindness hoti hai.

Agar woh logon ko nahi batayega ki woh dara hua hai, toh woh pareshan nahi honge. Agar woh apne past ke un hisson ko explain nahi karega jo use theek se yaad nahi, toh koi use un yaadon ko khodkar nikaalne ke liye nahi kahega. Agar woh apni problems ko itna chhota rakhe ki shayad woh ek din khud hi gayab ho jaayen.

Yeh plan bahut hi kharaab tha.

Ab use dheere-dheere iska ehsaas ho raha tha.

Raat ke 12:16 baje, usne Hanako Shimizu ki chat kholi.

Uski profile picture student council room ki khidki ki thi, jise sunset ke waqt liya gaya tha. Hanako ne kabhi kaha tha ki woh photo sentimental nahi hai. Aur phir bhi, woh use do saal se sambhaal kar rakhe hue thi.

Kaito ne ek message type kiya.

Phir delete kar diya.

Doosra type kiya.

Woh bhi delete kar diya.

Aakhir mein, usne likha:

Kaito: Kisi ne mujhe kuch photos bheji hain jo saalon pehle mere hospital mein rehne se judi hain. Mujhe lagta hai mujhe pata lagana chahiye ki tab kya hua tha. Main tumse help nahi maang raha, aur tum par reply karne ki koi responsibility nahi hai. Bas ab dobara koi zaroori baat chhupana nahi chahta tha.

Woh screen ko dekhta raha.

Phir usne send kar diya.

Poore ek minute tak kuch nahi hua.

Kaito ne phone neeche rakha, khada hua, kitchen tak gaya, apne room mein lauta, aur phir phone utha liya.

Koi reply nahi.

“Excellent,” usne budbudaaya. “Ek message bhejne ke baad bilkul normal reaction.”

Phone vibrate hua.

Hanako: Mujhe batane ke liye thank you.

Ek doosra message aaya.

Hanako: Main promise nahi kar sakti ki tumse baat karne par mujhe dard nahi hoga. Lekin main yeh promise kar sakti hoon ki mujhse poochhe bina tum yeh decide nahi karoge ki main kya handle kar sakti hoon.

Kaito ne use do baar padha.

Phir teesra message aaya.

Hanako: Sach ka pata lagao. Jo bhi jaanoge, agar mujhe batana chaho toh pehle mujhse poochhna. Us waqt main khud decide karungi.

Usne dheere se saans chhodi.

Yeh maafi nahi thi.

Yeh unke beete hue rishte mein lautne ka invitation bhi nahi tha.

Lekin yeh imaandaar tha.

Pehli baar, use samajh aaya ki sach bolne se happy ending ki guarantee nahi milti. Bas logon ko use chunne ka ek asli mauka milta hai.

Usne photo dobara kholi.

Hospital ka corridor. Uski maa. Peechhe Aoi. Woh purana naam jise woh peeche chhodne ki koshish karta raha tha.

Kal woh Aoi ko dhoondhega.

Aur is baar, sawaal woh khud poochega.

Chapter OneThe Girl Who Remembered Everything

Aoi Kanzaki ne usse public library mein milne ke liye haan kar di.

“Neutral ground,” usne phone par samjhaya. “Aur agar tumne mysterious banne ki acting shuru ki, toh main tumhe children's section mein bitha dungi.”

“Main mysterious acting nahi karta.”

“Tum ek baar bina kuch explain kiye teen saal ke liye gayab ho gaye the.”

“Woh alag baat thi.”

“Achha?”

Kaito ke paas koi jawaab nahi tha.

Agle din dopahar ko Aoi library ki unchi khidkiyon ke paas do drinks lekar uska intezaar kar rahi thi. Usne ek tea bottle uski taraf badhaayi.

“Tum ab woh coffee brand nahi peete,” usne kaha.

“Kabhi-kabhi peeta hoon.”

“Toh main bina wajah hi dramatic tareeke se galat thi.”

“Tumhe practice toh hai.”

Usne use ek nazar se dekha. Kaito ke honton par lagbhag muskaan aa gayi.

Ek pal ke liye, woh phir se do purane dost the—not woh ladka jo gayab ho gaya tha aur woh ladki jise peeche chhod diya gaya tha, balki do log jo ek hi summers ko yaad karte the.

Phir Aoi ne table par ek folder rakh diya.

“Maine copies sambhaal kar rakhi thi,” usne kaha. “Tumhari maa ne kaha tha, agar kabhi tumhe jaanne ka mann ho toh kaam aayengi.”

Kaito ne folder khola.

Usmein school photographs, appointment cards aur kuch haath se likhe notes the. Ek photo mein uska chhota wala version hospital bed par baitha tha aur camera ko gusse se dekh raha tha. Aoi uske paas khadi thi, uske sir ke peeche rabbit ears bana rahi thi.

“Mujhe yeh yaad hai,” usne kaha.

“Tumne mujhe hospital director se complain karne ki dhamki di thi.”

“Main serious tha.”

“Tum baarah saal ke the. Tumhe lagta tha hospital director saare hospitals ka principal hota hai.”

“Mere paas kaafi kuch chal raha tha.”

Aoi hans padi, aur us awaaz se Kaito ke seene mein jama kuch bojh halka ho gaya.

Phir woh aakhri page tak pahuncha: ek consent form ki photocopy, jis par uski maa aur ek doctor ke signatures the. Uske neeche uski apni handwriting mein ek note tha.

Mujhe yaad nahi ki maine agree kiya tha.

Kaito ki ungliyan paper par kas gayin.

“Maine kis baat ke liye agree kiya tha?”

Aoi ki muskaan gayab ho gayi.

“Woh nahi jo tum soch rahe ho.”

“Toh batao.”

Usne apne haath jod liye.

“Jab tum chhote the, tumhe ek serious illness thi. Tum har waqt dare rehte the, lekin tumhe yeh bilkul pasand nahi tha ki log tumhe naazuk samajhkar treat karein. Ek bahut mushkil raat ke baad, tumne apni maa se kaha tha ki tum woh ladka banna band karna chahte ho jiske liye sab log hamesha pareshan rehte hain.”

“Mujhe beemar rehna yaad hai. Lekin yeh kehna yaad nahi.”

“Tum bilkul thak chuke the. Dawa li hui thi aur neend bhi poori nahi ho rahi thi. Tumhari maa ne doctor se kaha tha ki jab tum thoda theek mehsoos karo, tab options dobara samjhaaye jaayen. Woh form ek treatment plan ke baare mein tha—not koi secret operation, na koi deal, aur na hi kisi ka tumhare choices chheen lena.”

Kaito signature ko dekhta raha.

“Aur woh note?”

“Tumne woh tab likha tha jab doctor ne sab kuch theek se samjhaya bhi nahi tha. Tum gusse mein the kyunki tumhe lag raha tha sabne tumhare liye pehle hi faisla kar liya hai.” Aoi ek pal ruki. “Tumhari maa ne use isliye sambhaal kar rakha, kyunki woh bhoolna nahi chahti thi ki tumhari apni zindagi ke decisions mein tumhe bhi shaamil hona zaroori hai.”

Usne woh sentence dobara padha.

Ab woh kisi saazish ka saboot nahi lag raha tha. Woh ek dare hue bachche ki awaaz lag rahi thi, jo chahta tha ki koi uski baat sune.

“Mere naam ka kya?” usne poochha.

“Transfer ke baad tumne Kaito naam use karna shuru kiya. Tumne kaha tha ki tum aisa naam chahte ho jise sunkar purane hospital ke log tumhe us din ki yaad se na dekhein jo unke liye sabse bura tha.” Aoi ne nazar neeche kar li. “Tumhari maa ne tum par force nahi kiya. Tumne khud choose kiya tha. Lekin baad mein, jab tumhe har detail yaad nahi rahi, tum sochne lage ki kya tumne kabhi kuch apni marzi se choose bhi kiya tha.”

Kaito kursi par peeche ho gaya.

Is mystery mein koi villain saamne nahi aaya tha.

Bas ek dara hua bachcha, ek maa jo use protect karne ki koshish kar rahi thi, aur ek dost jo kabhi samajh hi nahi paayi ki poori kahaani kaise samjhaaye.

“Tumne mujhe pehle kyun nahi bataya?”

“Tumhari maa ne mujhse kaha tha ki tumhe space doon. Shuru mein mujhe laga kuch hafton ki baat hai. Phir tumne jawaab dena band kar diya. Maine letters likhe. Calls kiye. Aakhir mein main gussa ho gayi.” Aoi ki awaaz narm pad gayi. “Maine khud ko samjha diya ki tum mujhe bhool gaye kyunki main tumhare liye itni important nahi thi.”

“Main tumhe nahi bhoola tha.”

“Tum mujhse baat karna bhool gaye the.”

Usne Aoi ki taraf dekha.

“Shayad woh aur bhi bura hai.”

“Tha,” usne bina kisi kadwahat ke kaha. “Lekin hamesha ke liye nahi.”

Photos milne ke baad pehli baar, Kaito ko laga ki past ab ek aisi shape le raha hai jise woh samajh sakta hai.

Perfect shape nahi. Aisi bhi nahi jisme koi regret na ho.

Lekin ab use samajhne ke liye kisi anjaan insaan ke threatening messages ki zaroorat nahi thi.

“Kya main apni maa se baat kar sakta hoon?” usne poochha.

Aoi ne sir hila diya.

“Aur uske baad,” usne kaha, “main tumse properly maafi maangna chahta hoon.”

“Lunch khila kar shuru kar sakte ho.”

“Bas yahi condition hai?”

“Nahi. Woh late fee hai.”

Kaito lagbhag hans diya.

“Theek hai.”

Chapter TwoA Mother's Explanation

Kaito ki maa ne bina beech mein roke uski poori baat suni.

Woh dono uske apartment ki chhoti dining table par baithe the. Wahi table jahan woh use dawa lene, homework khatam karne aur bukhaar ko chhoti si baat samajhne ka natak band karne ki yaad dilaya karti thi.

Jab Kaito ki baat khatam hui, usne purana consent form apni taraf kheench liya.

“Tum bade hue, tab mujhe tumhe aur samjhana chahiye tha,” usne kaha.

“Aapne kuch baatein samjhaayi toh thi.”

“Kaafi nahi. Mujhe laga tum apni zindagi ka woh hissa peeche chhodna chahte ho. Maine tumhari khamoshi ko tumhara pakka faisla samajh liya.”

Kaito paper ko dekhta raha.

“Mujhe laga kisi ne mere liye decisions liye the.”

“Mujhe pata hai.” Uski aankhon mein thakaan thi, lekin awaaz sthir thi. “Tum bachche the. Beemar the, dare hue the aur gusse mein bhi. Doctors ne humein kuch options diye the. Maine unse kaha tha ki final plan banane se pehle tumhare behtar mehsoos karne tak intezaar karein. Tum usmein shaamil the. Bas tumhe har conversation yaad nahi hai.”

“Aapne mujhe naam badalne kyun diya?”

“Kyunki tumne mujhse kaha tha. Tum ek nayi shuruaat chahte the, aur mujhe laga shayad isse tumhe madad milegi. Lekin mujhe yeh bhi samjhana chahiye tha ki nayi shuruaat ka matlab un sab logon ko mita dena nahi hota jo tumse pehle se pyaar karte the.”

Usne mushkil se apna gala saaf kiya.

“Kya aapko lagta tha main phir se beemar ho jaunga?”

“Har din.” Usne table ke paar haath badhaaya, phir ruk gayi—use mauka dete hue ki woh khud uska haath pakadna chahe. Kaito ne uska haath thaam liya. “Lekin main kabhi nahi chahti thi ki tum apni poori zindagi aise jiyo jaise beemar hona hi tumhari eklauti pehchaan ho.”

Saalon tak Kaito ko laga tha ki uski maa koi aisa sach chhupa rahi hai jo use be-bas mehsoos kara dega. Lekin woh toh bas us mushkil waqt ke tukdon ko sambhaal kar baithi thi, aur intezaar kar rahi thi ki Kaito kab unhe dekhne ke liye ready hoga.

“I'm sorry,” usne kaha.

Uski maa ne uska haath dabaaya.

“Darr jaane ke liye tumhe maafi maangne ki zaroorat nahi.”

“Lekin sabko apne se door karne ke liye maafi maangni chahiye.”

“Haan,” usne kaha. “Woh toh karni chahiye.”

Uski seedhi baat sunkar Kaito hairaan reh gaya.

Phir woh muskuraayi.
